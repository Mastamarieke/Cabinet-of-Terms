## Van Veeren, Moerking & Dryer (2026) — Taking the red pill: conspiracy theories, gender, and the "elusive epistemologies" of the "manosphere"

**Van Veeren, E., Moerking, E. & Dryer, H. (2026).** Taking the red pill: conspiracy theories, gender, and the "elusive epistemologies" of the "manosphere". *International Feminist Journal of Politics*, 28(2), 437–470. https://doi.org/10.1080/14616742.2026.2646522

---

### What this source contributes

The authors read the anti-feminist red pill as a "Matrix conspiracy theory". Building on Harambam and Aupers' work on how conspiracy theorists legitimise their truth claims through futuristic imaginaries, they show the role of popular culture and intertextuality in producing the "common sense" of these beliefs. In a discourse analysis of a representative sample of red-pill YouTube videos, they find that the communities draw on the "image bank" of *The Matrix* far more extensively than the pill metaphor alone, and in ways that matter for legitimising violence: to rank masculinities, to tell a story of aggrieved sexual entitlement against "feminist oppression", and to call for a violent "return" to a traditional gendered, sexual and racial order.

### Analytical function in Red Pill

This source shows that the borrowing from *The Matrix* is not a single metaphor but a whole visual and narrative vocabulary. It connects the entry to [[The Matrix]] as more than a footnote, and supports the Friction's point that the red pill is a structure, not just an image.
