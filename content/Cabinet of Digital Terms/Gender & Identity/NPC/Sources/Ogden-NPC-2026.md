## Ogden (2026) — ASMR NPC

**Ogden, M.K. (2026).** ASMR NPC. *Media Theory*, 10(1), 101–124. https://doi.org/10.70064/mt.v10i1.1369

---

### What this source contributes

A study of ASMR NPC streams through the idea of "possession". Ogden first looks at popular streams in which performers, like the non-player characters of role-playing games, are stuck in loops of repeated movements and phrases, and links this to popular commentary that reads the streams as objectification and loss of agency. Drawing on research on "digital possession", he then complicates that reading: the possession of one person or group by another is absorbed into a more general possession tied to the infrastructure of audiovisual platforms under contemporary capitalism.

### Analytical function in NPC

The entry's Friction says the streaming version reverses the insult, with viewers deciding what the character does. This source keeps that from becoming too simple: the viewers do not hold the controls alone; streamer and audience are both moved by the platform's loops.
