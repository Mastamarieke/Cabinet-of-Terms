## Gallagher & Topinka (2023) — The politics of the NPC meme: Reactionary subcultural practice and vernacular theory

**Gallagher, R. & Topinka, R. (2023).** The politics of the NPC meme: Reactionary subcultural practice and vernacular theory. *Big Data & Society*. https://doi.org/10.1177/20539517231172422

---

### What this source contributes

"NPC" comes from videogame culture, where it means a computer-controlled character run by its programming. By 2018 it had become shorthand in right-wing subcultural spaces for people supposedly incapable of thinking for themselves, and by the autumn of that year those spaces were full of memes accusing liberals and leftists of parroting progressive catchphrases. In mid-October, before the US midterms, Twitter banned more than 1,000 NPC role-play accounts created by Trump supporters, citing disinformation. Combining computational analysis with close reading, the authors trace the meme's trajectory and show how mainstream attention both amplified it and weakened its charge in the spaces where it began. They also argue that, while it has carried antidemocratic bigotry, the meme may offer a vocabulary for theorising the effects of communicative capitalism.

### Analytical function in NPC

The source for the corrected Origin (the 2018 removals were about disinformation, not dehumanisation) and for the Appeal's "true thing": the meme names something people recognise about talking in borrowed lines.
