## 1. Platforming the Joe Rogan Experience: Cancel Culture, Comedy, and Infrastructure

**Author:** Nicola Bozzi
**Year:** 2024
**Type:** Academic
**Publisher:** Television & New Media
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/15274764241277476

### What this source contributes

A cultural critique of *The Joe Rogan Experience* as an adaptive platform shaped by communicative capitalism and Rogan's comedian ethos: his relationship with Spotify and "cancel" discourse, his format for platforming fellow comedians on YouTube, and his relationship with Elon Musk.

## 2. Inequality in Popular Podcasts? An Examination of Gender & Race/Ethnicity

**Author:** Annenberg Inclusion Initiative (USC)
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Research report
**Publisher:** USC Annenberg
**URL:** https://assets.uscannenberg.org/docs/aii-inequality-Podcasts-2025-11-06.pdf

### What this source contributes

Of the hosts of the top 100 US podcasts of 2024, 64% were men and 77% white; across 592 popular podcasts, 66.3% male and 79.2% white; only 6.6% of shows had a woman of colour among the hosts. Figures as reported by NPR and other outlets in November 2025.

## 4. The Future of Podcasting is Women

**Author:** Meg Davies & Joanna Aveillan
**Year:** 2026
**Type:** Industry research
**Publisher:** Acast
**URL:** https://www.acast.com/en-gb/blog/the-future-of-podcasting-is-women

### What this source contributes

Acast's European research (31 March 2026): 40% of European listeners believe the industry fails to represent women adequately as hosts; 33% are not exposed to enough content hosted by women, noting that algorithms tend to favour male creators.
