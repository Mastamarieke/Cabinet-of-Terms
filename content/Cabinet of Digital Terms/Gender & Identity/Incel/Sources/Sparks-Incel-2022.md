## Sparks, Zidenberg & Olver (2022) — Involuntary Celibacy: A Review of Incel Ideology and Experiences with Dating, Rejection, and Associated Mental Health and Emotional Sequelae

**Sparks, B., Zidenberg, A.M. & Olver, M.E. (2022).** Involuntary Celibacy: A Review of Incel Ideology and Experiences with Dating, Rejection, and Associated Mental Health and Emotional Sequelae. *Current Psychiatry Reports*, 24, 731–740. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11920-022-01382-9

### What this source contributes

A review of the small body of research that has recruited incels directly, set against the much larger literature that reads only forum posts and manifestos. The authors point to a considerable tension between how incels describe themselves and how they are characterised from outside. Forum analyses show openly misogynistic dialogue; interview studies suggest this does not represent most members' experience, and one moderator describes how inflammatory posts quickly come to dominate a discussion.

The review gathers the figures that make the incel experience concrete: depression and anxiety far above population rates, heightened rejection sensitivity, self-esteem contingent on relationships, and profound isolation, with only a third reporting at least one friend. It also records what the forums give in return, 70% reporting a sense of belonging and 75% feeling understood, and it puts the violence in proportion: roughly one in ten admires the men who carried out attacks, and existing models of violence cannot tell violent from non-violent incels.

### Analytical function in Incel

This source carries the Appeal, and it does so with numbers instead of sympathy. It is also the entry's evidence that the forums answer a real need and that the answer and the harm come from the same place. The tension it names between self-perception and outside characterisation is the review-level version of the entry's opening claim: that the word does two jobs at once.

### About the author(s)

Sparks and Olver work in forensic psychology at the University of Saskatchewan; Zidenberg at Lakehead University. The same group published a follow-up on loneliness among incels in 2023 (see the secondary bundle).
