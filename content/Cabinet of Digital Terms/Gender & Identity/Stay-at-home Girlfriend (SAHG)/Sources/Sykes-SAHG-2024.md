## Sykes (2024) — From 'girlboss' to #stayathomegirlfriend: The romanticisation of domestic labour on TikTok

**Sykes, I. (2024).** From 'girlboss' to #stayathomegirlfriend: The romanticisation of domestic labour on TikTok. *European Journal of Cultural Studies*, 28(3), 830–848. https://doi.org/10.1177/13675494241285643

---

### What this source contributes

Since the Covid-19 pandemic, romanticised images of domestic work have spread on social media, especially TikTok, with repetitive routines, catharsis and feminine aesthetics. The #stayathomegirlfriend trend, rooted in tradwife ideology and its "return" to a male-breadwinner model, presents domesticity as an escape from the "double shift" and as a backlash to popular feminism's call to "lean in". Housework becomes an aesthetic act of self-care for a generation that watched their mothers struggle in the labour market and were told to become "girlbosses" themselves. In a thematic analysis of popular videos, Sykes shows that stay-at-home girlfriends unwittingly mimic the popular-feminist language of empowerment they seem to reject.

### Analytical function in Stay-at-home Girlfriend (SAHG)

The entry's main source for the trend's roots (pandemic, tradwife ideology), its appeal (escape from the double shift) and its contradiction (empowerment language used for dependency).
