## Mech (1999) — Alpha status, dominance, and division of labor in wolf packs

**Mech, L.D. (1999).** Alpha status, dominance, and division of labor in wolf packs. *Canadian Journal of Zoology*, 77(8), 1196–1203. https://doi.org/10.1139/z99-099

---

### What this source contributes

The prevailing image of a wolf pack, Mech writes, is a group whose members are always competing for dominance, held in check by an "alpha" pair. But most research on the social dynamics of packs had been done on non-natural assortments of captive wolves. Drawing on the literature and thirteen summers of observing free-living wolves on Ellesmere Island, he concludes that the typical pack is a family: the adult parents guide the group in a division of labour, the mother mainly caring for and defending the pups, the father mainly foraging and providing food.

### Analytical function in Alpha Male

This is the correction the ideology never took on board, by the researcher whose 1970 book had made the alpha famous. It grounds the Origin and the entry's closing image: in the wild, the wolf at the head of the pack is a parent, and its work is care.

### About the author(s)

L. David Mech is a wolf researcher. On his own website he writes that his book *The Wolf* (1970) helped ingrain the alpha concept, and that wolves who lead packs are simply the parents.
