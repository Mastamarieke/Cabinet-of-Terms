semantic_landscape: |
  **Tradwife** sits in **Gender & Identity** where two lines meet. One comes from **SMV (Sexual Market Value)**, the idea that men and women trade value on a market: the tradwife offers housework, children and deference, the **Provider** offers money, and each role needs the other. The other line comes from **Purity Culture**: the clean body, the pure household, the pure family.

  Follow the market to the men's side and it returns in **Looksmaxxing** and **Sigma Male**. Follow it a step younger and you reach **Stay-at-home Girlfriend (SAHG)**, the same deal without the wedding ring. **Womanosphere** is where conservative women build their own media around it, and **Cleanfluencer** shows the shop: under one cleaning channel's videos, *"Jesus, Mom, Wife, Cook, Clean, Organize"* is followed by *"Amazon links"*. On an ordinary webshop the slogans are sold as shirts: *"Feed The Patriarchy"*, *"Feminine Not Feminist"*.

  Further out lies the politics the kitchen does not show. For some accounts, having children is resistance to the **Great Replacement**, and **Pro-natalism** takes a racial form; the **Wellness-to-Alt-Right Pipeline** is the route there from softer content.

  At the end are the women: Alena Kate Pettitt, who never chose the word; Hannah Neeleman, who was given it by a newspaper; and the women who post that they have left.

***A woman in a floral dress kneads bread and tells you she gave up her job to serve her husband. Is she at work right now?***

**Literal meaning:** **[[Tradwife]]** is a blend of *traditional* and *wife* ([Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/tradwife "Read the Wiktionary entry for tradwife.")). The tradition is a particular one: Wiktionary places it in the Western middle-class femininity of the mid-twentieth century, the housewife of the 1950s. Shortened to *trad*, a way of life becomes a label you can put in a bio or a hashtag. The word is used for women who take on this role as a values-driven lifestyle choice, keeping house, raising children and deferring to a male [[Provider|provider]], and for women who show that life online as [[Influencer|influencers]] in the [[Creator Economy|creator economy]]. ==How many women live as tradwives is unknown, and research follows the ones who post== ([[Sykes-Tradwife-2024|Sykes & Hopner, 2024]]: thirty-six accounts, each chosen because it had at least 10,000 followers). ==A Dutch woman of 23 put the difference plainly: "There's always been a group of people, like religious people, who were already doing this anyway. Others are now just selling it online"== ([[Heldoorn-Tradwife-2026|Heldoorn et al., 2026]]: interviews with sixteen Dutch women about tradwife videos, 2024). In 2025 the ==Cambridge Dictionary==[^cambridge] added the word.

**Origin:** The word first appeared in a newspaper in June 2018, when [[Secondary-Tradwife#1. The Housewives of White Supremacy|Annie Kelly (2018)]] wrote in *The New York Times* about "the housewives of white supremacy", women whose homemaking content carried white nationalist ideas. Its first public face was British. In January 2020 a BBC film showed the homemaker and author ==Alena Kate Pettitt==[^pettitt] ironing her husband's shirts, "submitting to and spoiling my husband like it's 1959". In July 2024 the *Sunday Times Magazine* introduced Hannah Neeleman of [Ballerina Farm](https://www.instagram.com/ballerinafarm/ "See the Ballerina Farm account on Instagram.") as "the queen of the trad wives" ([[Secondary-Tradwife#2. Meet the queen of the 'trad wives' (and her eight children)|Agnew, 2024]]). ==She told a New York Times reporter that she had never heard the word before the paper contacted her== ([[Heldoorn-Tradwife-2026|Heldoorn et al., 2026]]: citing Malone Kircher in The New York Times, 2024), and ==the argument that followed==[^neeleman] made **[[Tradwife|tradwife]]** known far beyond the women it was first used for.

> Domestic femininity performed as aspirational content, where the rejection of professional identity is distributed as a lifestyle brand.

**The Appeal:** **[[Tradwife]]** content answers a real tiredness. ==Women gained ground at work and much less at home, where men still do less than half of the housework and much less of the planning== ([[Secondary-Tradwife#14. Not the Solution We Proposed: Feminist Psychological Insights into the Tradwife Phenomenon|Betz et al., 2026]]: the introduction to a special issue of seven studies on tradwives). The tradwife names feminism as the cause and offers a way out: stop trying to have it all. ==During the pandemic, when people baked bread and planted gardens, feeds of cottagecore and homemaking carried many women on to tradwife videos== ([[Secondary-Tradwife#14. Not the Solution We Proposed: Feminist Psychological Insights into the Tradwife Phenomenon|Betz et al., 2026]]: drawing on Hopner and Sykes, 2026). The kitchen is calm, and housework that usually goes unseen is shown as worth something. For some women it is also an economic calculation, the pattern that [[Hypergamy]] names: marrying a partner with more money or status. ==Even in Norway, where women and men work in roughly equal numbers, a husband on average has better earning prospects than his wife, couples then tend to put his career first, and that may explain part of the pay gap that remains== ([[Almas-Hypergamy-2023|Almås et al., 2023]]: register data on everyone born in Norway between 1952 and 1975, earning prospects measured by the parents' income).

**The Friction:** She is at work: the video that tells women they need not earn is how she earns. ==Of thirty-six tradwife accounts followed for ten months, all but one made money, through adverts, brand deals and small businesses, such as a course on growing your social media without it taking over your life as a homemaker== ([[Sykes-Tradwife-2024|Sykes & Hopner, 2024]]: accounts on Instagram, Facebook, X, YouTube, TikTok and smaller platforms, observed in 2022). The same study found the soft and the hard version in one account. ==On Instagram and Facebook, where moderation is stricter, the women shared homemaking and #tradlife, while on X, YouTube, Rumble and Gab the same women argued against feminism and trans rights and tried to radicalise their followers== ([[Sykes-Tradwife-2024|Sykes & Hopner, 2024]]: the most controversial posts drew the most reactions). That makes the calm kitchen the way in, and the [[Wellness-to-Alt-Right Pipeline]], the route from lifestyle content to far-right ideas, a strategy rather than a coincidence. ==Dutch women who watched these videos noticed that she earns while her message is that her husband should be the provider, and asked who could afford the life without family money, yet none of them connected it to the misogyny and white supremacism that research warns about== ([[Heldoorn-Tradwife-2026|Heldoorn et al., 2026]]: sixteen women aged 21 to 76, after watching the NPO series Girls of Tradition).

**Why This Matters:** Once you know the word, a homemaking video is also a business, and sometimes a political message. You notice it when a video about baking bread ends with a link to a course, or with a remark about what "real women" do. Then you can ask who earns from it, and whether the woman in it chose this life and respects women who choose otherwise. The tiredness it answers is real, and feminism answers it differently: ==an income of your own, so that you can still stand on your own feet if your life takes a different course== ([[Heldoorn-Tradwife-2026|Heldoorn et al., 2026]]: why the Dutch women who rejected the trend valued work and study), and ==care at home that men share, because it matters to everyone== ([[Secondary-Tradwife#14. Not the Solution We Proposed: Feminist Psychological Insights into the Tradwife Phenomenon|Betz et al., 2026]]: valuing care in a feminist way means men take it on too). In the Netherlands that is slowly happening: ==mothers who had their first child around 2017 earned a third less from work six years later, where mothers twenty years earlier earned more than half less, and nine in ten fathers now take birth leave== ([[Secondary-Tradwife#17. Emancipatiemonitor 2026|CBS, 2026]]: Emancipatiemonitor, Statistics Netherlands). ==Some women who tried the life now post about leaving it== ([[Secondary-Tradwife#14. Not the Solution We Proposed: Feminist Psychological Insights into the Tradwife Phenomenon|Betz et al., 2026]]: on Hopner and Sykes's study of women who de-tradded).
