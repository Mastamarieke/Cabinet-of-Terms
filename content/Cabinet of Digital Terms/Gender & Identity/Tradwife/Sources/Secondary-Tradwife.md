## 1. The Housewives of White Supremacy

**Author:** Annie Kelly
**Year:** 2018
**Type:** Investigative journalism
**Publisher:** The New York Times
**URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/2018/06/01/opinion/sunday/tradwives-women-alt-right.html

### What this source contributes

The first substantial journalistic exploration of the tradwife movement as part of the alt-right. Kelly documents how one aesthetic of domesticity and female submission runs continuously from mainstream presentation to explicitly white-nationalist content, including "white baby fever" as a demographic project. Essential to the Friction layer, because it establishes that the spectrum is structural rather than accidental.

## 2. Meet the queen of the 'trad wives' (and her eight children)

**Author:** Megan Agnew
**Year:** 2024
**Type:** Journalism / long-form profile
**Publisher:** The Sunday Times Magazine
**URL:** https://www.thetimes.com/magazines/the-sunday-times-magazine/article/meet-the-queen-of-the-trad-wives-and-her-eight-children-plfr50cgk
**Published:** 20 July 2024
**Access:** paywalled

### What this source contributes

The profile that made the term mainstream. Agnew visited Hannah Neeleman, known online as Ballerina Farm, on the family's 328-acre Utah farm in July 2024. The standfirst set the terms of the argument that followed: "Is this an empowering new model of womanhood — or a hammer blow for feminism".

Two things make it more than a case study. First, Neeleman had not called herself a tradwife; earlier New York Times coverage had framed her as a resilient woman competing in a beauty pageant shortly after giving birth. The label was applied from outside and she then had to answer it, which she did in two Instagram posts. Second, the article is measurable as a diffusion event: Founti (2025) shows through Google Trends that searches for "Ballerina Farm" and for **"tradwife" itself** spiked on 21 July 2024, the day after publication.

A follow-up piece, "My day with the trad wife queen and what it taught me", appeared on 29 July 2024.

### Status

Paywalled at the publisher. Copies circulate on document-sharing sites; those are unauthorised reproductions, may be removed without notice, and are not cited here.

### Related entries

- [[Tradwife]] — the naming moment at which the term entered general use, and the label as something assigned rather than claimed
- [[Highlight Reel]] — the gap between the curated feed and the reported day

## 3. A Peek Behind the Ballerina Farm Facade Is Bleak as Hell

**Author:** Kady Ruth Ashcraft
**Year:** 2024
**Type:** Journalism / commentary
**Publisher:** Jezebel
**URL:** https://www.jezebel.com/a-peek-behind-the-ballerina-farm-facade-is-bleak-as-hell

### What this source contributes

Published four days after Agnew's profile, and the clearest example of the oppositional reading. Ashcraft draws out what the profile records rather than states: that Agnew could not obtain an answer from Neeleman "without her being corrected, interrupted or answered for by either her husband or a child"; that Daniel Neeleman wanted marriage and pregnancy within three months of meeting in 2011, when Hannah was 21 and had asked to date for a year so she could finish her studies; that there is no childcare; and Daniel's own remark that his wife sometimes "gets so ill from exhaustion that she can't get out of bed for a week."

The quotation the piece turns on is Neeleman's answer to whether this life had always been her dream: "No. My goal was New York City. I left home at 17 and I was so excited to get there... And I was going to be a ballerina. I was a good ballerina."

What makes the article analytically useful is that it refuses the easy conclusion: "to paint Neeleman as purely a victim absolves her from her active participation in perpetuating the fantasy of a tradwife, even at her own detriment." Ashcraft holds constraint and complicity together rather than choosing one, and argues the content is designed to make other women feel inadequate.

- [[Tradwife]] — the oppositional reading, and the refusal to resolve the persona into either victimhood or agency
- [[Comparison Culture]] — content that produces inadequacy in its audience by design

## 4. The thorn in feminism's side: black feminist reconceptualization and defence of #tradwives and the #tradwife movement

**Author:** Laura Jane Bower
**Year:** 2024
**Type:** Academic article, peer-reviewed
**Publisher:** Journal of Gender Studies (Durham University)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/09589236.2024.2423198

### What this source contributes

Traces the construction of the tradwife as anti-feminist populism through a black feminist lens, and proposes a typology of three sub-types: the nostalgic tradwife, the former feminist, and the Southern Belle. Bower examines how the "unassuming hyperfeminine aesthetic" allows tradculture to carry alt-right ideology to an everyday audience, and argues that some tradwives fail to consider how nostalgic constructions of traditional femininity reproduce colonialist discourses along gendered, ethnic and class lines.

The article is both a defence and a critique, which is the reason to keep it. It rejects a certain dismissal of tradwives while holding the movement to account on grounds of race and colonial history that the other sources do not raise.

### Status

Assessed from the abstract and the published typology. The full text is behind publisher and repository protections that blocked retrieval, so the summary above has not been checked against the complete article.

### Related entries

- [[Tradwife]] — a three-part typology and the colonial dimension of the nostalgia
- [[Purity Culture]] — the racial and colonial content of "traditional" femininity

## 5. Alphas, Betas, and Incels: Theorizing the Masculinities of the Manosphere

**Author:** Debbie Ging
**Year:** 2019
**Type:** Academic article, peer-reviewed
**Publisher:** Men and Masculinities (SAGE)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/1097184x17706401

### What this source contributes

The first systematic academic analysis of the manosphere as an ecosystem of masculinity ideologies, with SMV (Sexual Market Value) as its organising principle. For this entry the relevant point is that the same market logic driving men toward looksmaxxing and alpha dominance places women on the other side of the transaction: the tradwife as the female mirror term of the provider, optimised for maximum mate value.

### Status

Peer-reviewed, and a primary source in the [[Sigma Male]] and [[Looksmaxxing]] entries. Held here as secondary because the article is about the manosphere rather than about tradwives; it supplies a genealogical frame by extension rather than direct evidence. Replaced as this entry's second primary by Sykes & Hopner (2024), which studies tradwives themselves.

- [[Tradwife]] — SMV logic as the principle linking tradwife to the manosphere rather than opposing them
- [[Looksmaxxing]] — the male route through the same market logic
- [[SMV (Sexual Market Value)]] — the organising principle itself

## 10. The performance and monetization of gender stereotypes through the case of Hannah Neeleman

**Author:** Filippia Founti
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Master's thesis (Media and Communication Studies, two-year), supervisor Bojana Romic; 98 pages, not peer-reviewed
**Publisher:** Malmö University, School of Arts and Communication (K3), DiVA
**URL:** https://mau.diva-portal.org/smash/record.jsf?pid=diva2:1998859
**Access:** open access

### What this source contributes

A case study of Hannah Neeleman's performance of femininity, motherhood and domesticity across Instagram and the Sunday Times, read through Goffman's dramaturgy and Hall's three reading positions. Its usable finding for the entry is the split reception: on Neeleman's own Instagram, with a self-selected audience, agreeing readings dominate; under the *Sunday Times* profile, oppositional ones. The same performance, two publics, two readings. The thesis also documents, with Google Trends, that searches for "tradwife" peaked on 21 July 2024, the day after the profile.

Its bibliography led to Sykes & Hopner (2024) and Bower (2024), both now in this entry's sources.

### Related entries

- [[Tradwife]] — the split reception in Friction; the search peak after Agnew's profile

## 11. Ballerina Farm — the Instagram account

**Author:** Hannah Neeleman
**Year:** 2017–ongoing
**Type:** Instagram account; the phenomenon itself
**Publisher:** Instagram
**URL:** https://www.instagram.com/ballerinafarm/

### What this source contributes

The primary material every other source here reads: the farm, the eight children, the sourdough, the pageant, the two posts that answered the tradwife label after Agnew's profile. It is also the public that Founti (2025) contrasts with the newspaper's: a self-selected audience of millions in which agreeing readings dominate, where the same performance under the *Sunday Times* headline drew the oppositional ones. To be read as a text, not as a source of facts about the family: what it shows is what it chooses to show.

### Status

Artefact: the phenomenon as product, not a source about it. Followers, posts and captions change; the account may not look tomorrow as it does today, and no snapshot is kept here.
