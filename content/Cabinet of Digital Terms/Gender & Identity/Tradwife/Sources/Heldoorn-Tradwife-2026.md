## Heldoorn, Hendrix, Vandeberg & Daalmans (2026) — #TradWives: A Qualitative Meaning-Making Study of Gendered Norms, Moral Appropriation, and Feminism

**Heldoorn, P., Hendrix, J., Vandeberg, L. & Daalmans, S. (2026).** #TradWives: A Qualitative Meaning-Making Study of Gendered Norms, Moral Appropriation, and Feminism. *Media and Communication*, 14, article 11927. https://doi.org/10.17645/mac.11927

---

### What this source contributes

A study of the audience rather than the makers. In spring 2024 the authors interviewed sixteen Dutch women aged 21 to 76, for about an hour each, after all of them had watched the first episode of the NPO series *Girls of Tradition* (2023); six already knew tradwife content. Using reflexive thematic analysis and Stuart Hall's model of encoding and decoding, they describe how the women made sense of the trend.

The women did not simply accept or reject it. They valued every woman's right to choose and kept judging the choice at the same time, an ambivalence the authors call ambivalent acceptance. They saw tradwife videos as a lifestyle among others, and as a reaction against progressive change. They noticed the contradiction in the business: the message is that the husband is the breadwinner, while the woman making the content earns from it, which the videos leave unsaid. They asked who could afford the life without family money or a wealthy husband, and valued study and work because they keep a woman independent if her life takes a different course. Some would accept the tradwife life as choice feminism, on conditions: a conscious choice, respect for women who choose otherwise, and no attempt to convert them. Others held that submitting to a husband can never be feminist.

What the women did not do is connect the trend to misogyny or white supremacy, the consequences that research and the press write about most. The authors note the limits: a convenience sample of mostly progressive women, most of whom did not watch tradwife content regularly.

### Analytical function in Tradwife

The second primary, beside Sykes & Hopner (2024): where they show what the accounts do, this study shows what viewers see. It carries three places in the entry. In Literal meaning, a participant's sentence separates the women who have always lived this way from those who sell it online. In The Friction, the finding that critical viewers saw the money but not the politics makes Sykes & Hopner's platform calibration concrete: the soft version works, even on viewers who disagree with it. In Why This Matters, the women's reason for valuing work and study is the feminist answer to the tiredness the trend speaks to.

### About the author(s)

The authors are at the Behavioural Science Institute of Radboud University, Nijmegen; Serena Daalmans is the corresponding author.
