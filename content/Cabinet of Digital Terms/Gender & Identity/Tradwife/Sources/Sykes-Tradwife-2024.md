## Sykes & Hopner (2024) — Tradwives: Right-Wing Social Media Influencers

**Sykes, S. & Hopner, V. (2024).** Tradwives: Right-Wing Social Media Influencers. *Journal of Contemporary Ethnography*, 53(4), 453–487. https://doi.org/10.1177/08912416241246273

---

### What this source contributes

A netnographic study of thirty-six tradwife social media profiles, thirty-one individual and five group accounts, observed over ten months in 2022. Participants were predominantly American, with others from Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and New Zealand, spread across age bands under 25, 25 to 45, and over 50. The authors organise their findings into four components: the tradwife landscape, cross-platform influencing, "feminine not feminist", and the tradwife side-hustle.

The most consequential finding concerns **platform calibration**. The same influencer varies the format, message and extremity of her content according to how strictly a platform moderates. On Facebook and Instagram the content is lifestyle and homemaking, tagged #tradlife, with right-wing messaging kept implicit and a vocabulary of contentment, security and happiness. On Twitter/X, YouTube, Rumble and Gab the same women post explicit ideological argument, attempt redpilling through long-form video, and address gender essentialism and anti-trans positions directly. Engagement concentrated around the most controversial material.

Within the landscape the authors distinguish religious tradwives, the largest group and present across every ideological position; political tradwives, who use complementarianism to justify their own public careers; militia tradwives; counterculture tradwives, the most overt in recruitment; and pagan tradwives, who frame white racial superiority as archetypal divine femininity. Among Alt-Right religious profiles the anti-abortion position is tied explicitly to Great Replacement conspiracy.

The authors summarise the movement as "globalizing anti-globalism and modernizing the anti-modern", and record that what all thirty-six profiles shared was a rejection of feminism combined with identification as feminine rather than feminist.

### Analytical function in Tradwife

This is the empirical spine the entry previously lacked. It replaces argument by analogy with observation: the soft Instagram version and the explicit Gab version are not separate populations but the same accounts adjusting to their audience and their moderators.

That finding changes what the aesthetic layer means. A kitchen video is not the harmless end of a spectrum whose other end belongs to different people. It is the entry point of a distribution strategy, which is what makes the pipeline into radicalised content a mechanism rather than a coincidence of adjacency.

### About the authors

**Veronica Hopner** is based at Massey University, New Zealand, where her research covers political psychology, extremism and radicalisation. The article is co-authored with S. Sykes.
