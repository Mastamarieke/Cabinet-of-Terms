## 1. Sex differences in human mate preferences: Evolutionary hypotheses tested in 37 cultures

**Author:** David M. Buss
**Year:** 1989
**Type:** Academic
**Publisher:** Behavioral and Brain Sciences
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00023992

### What this source contributes

The study the evolutionary version of the claim leans on. Using questionnaires from 37 samples in 33 countries (N = 10,047), Buss found that women valued cues to resource acquisition in a partner more highly than men did, and men valued signs of reproductive capacity more than women did, which he reads as the result of different evolutionary pressures.

## 2. The origins of sex differences in human behavior: Evolved dispositions versus social roles

**Author:** Alice H. Eagly & Wendy Wood
**Year:** 1999
**Type:** Academic
**Publisher:** American Psychologist
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.6.408

### What this source contributes

Sets the explanation from evolved dispositions against an explanation from social roles: sex differences in what people look for in a partner as an outcome of the positions men and women hold in society. Listed here as the main counter-position to Buss; the entry does not quote its findings.

## 4. TikTok Dating Coaches Claim They Can Help You "Marry Up"

**Author:** Ade Onibada
**Year:** 2022
**Type:** Journalism
**Publisher:** BuzzFeed News
**URL:** https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/adeonibada/dating-coaches-hypergamy-millionaire-tiktok

### What this source contributes

Reports on TikTok dating coaches who teach women to marry wealthy men, one charging $150 a session, at a time when #hypergamy had 126 million views on TikTok. A sociologist at the University of Michigan calls the strategy "unlikely to be a terribly successful strategy", because women in the US now graduate from college at higher rates than men. BuzzFeed News closed in 2023; the article is readable through the Internet Archive.
