***The claim: women always want someone higher on the hierarchy. The evidence: personal anecdote, presented as evolutionary biology.***

**Literal meaning:** **Hypergamy**, from Greek *hyper* (above) and *gamos* (marriage), is the sociological term for marrying someone of higher social status. In the **manosphere** it has become a claim about every woman: she is wired to want a man with more money and status than her own, and will trade up as soon as one comes along. In the same vocabulary the man she is said to want is the [[Alpha Male]] — the dominant man at the top of the hierarchy — and the man who pays is the [[Provider]]. The word keeps the scholarly sound of the Greek and changes its subject: *gamos* was marriage between families and classes, while the manosphere uses it for dating.

**Origin:** Anthropologists used the term for marriage patterns, particularly in caste societies. The manosphere stripped it of that history and recast it as biology. It reached a wide audience in 2014, when the Isla Vista killer posted a chart that [[Secondary-Hypergamy|David Futrelle (2014)]] described as "his chilling spin on a manosphere meme depicting supposed female 'hypergamy'".

> A sociological term describing marriage patterns, captured and redeployed as a universal biological law to explain men's romantic failures.

**The Appeal:** **Hypergamy** explains rejection from the outside: not *I am unsuitable* but *women are programmed this way*, with a prescription attached: become a [[High Value Man|high value man]]. And the pattern it points to is real, which is why it convinces. For some women it is also a reasonable choice: where a career runs into a ceiling, a partner who provides, as in the [[Tradwife]], [[Stay-at-home Girlfriend (SAHG)|stay-at-home girlfriend]] and [[Provider]] models, offers security that work does not.

**The Friction:** What is contested is not the pattern but the drive. Where a glass ceiling keeps women's earnings lower, a partner who earns more is one of the few routes upward left open, and the pattern feeds itself: even in Norway it holds, and once a household organises around the higher earner, her earnings fall further behind ([[Almas-Hypergamy-2023|Almås et al., 2023]]). As the manosphere uses it, the claim cannot be refuted: every couple in which he earns more confirms it, and every other couple is explained away. [[SMV (Sexual Market Value)]] makes it operational: if attractiveness is a score, **hypergamy** describes how women shop. [[Incel]] forums add that dating apps speed it up ([[Preston-Hypergamy-2021|Preston, Halpin and Maguire, 2021]]), and [[Womanosphere]] turns it into strategy; on TikTok in 2022, [[Influencer|influencers]] charged $150 a session to teach women how to marry rich ([[Secondary-Hypergamy|Onibada, 2022]]). Those coaches earned from the sessions and from the views, as creators do in the [[Creator Economy]].

**Why This Matters:** **Hypergamy** makes visible how scientific-sounding vocabulary can turn a pattern in society into a fact about every woman. Once the claim is biological, no counter-evidence counts, and the circumstances in which women actually choose drop out of view.
