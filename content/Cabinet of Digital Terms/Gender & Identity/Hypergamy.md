***The claim: women always want someone higher on the hierarchy. The evidence: personal anecdote, presented as evolutionary biology.***

**Literal meaning:** **Hypergamy** — from Greek *hyper* (above) and *gamos* (marriage) — in its sociological sense describes the practice of marrying someone of higher social status. In **manosphere** discourse, it is redeployed as a universal female biological drive: women are hardwired to seek partners with higher status, resources, and dominance than themselves, and to continuously seek upgrades. The claim is presented as evolutionary biology. Something shifts in the second half of the word: *gamos* is marriage, an arrangement between families and classes, while the manosphere uses **hypergamy** for dating and sex, for one woman choosing one man. The word keeps the scholarly sound of the Greek and changes its subject.

**Origin:** The sociological concept of **hypergamy** has been used in anthropology and sociology to describe marriage patterns, particularly in caste societies. The **manosphere** appropriation strips the historical and cultural specificity from the concept and presents it as a universal biological law. The claim draws selectively on evolutionary psychology — specifically sexual selection theory — while ignoring the substantial research complicating or contradicting it. The word reached a wider public in May 2014, after the Isla Vista killings, when David Futrelle of *We Hunted the Mammoth* described a chart the killer had posted as "his chilling spin on a manosphere meme depicting supposed female 'hypergamy'".

> A sociological term describing marriage patterns, captured and redeployed as a universal biological law to explain men's romantic failures.

**The Appeal:** **Hypergamy** provides an explanation for rejection that locates the cause externally: it is not that I am unattractive or unsuitable, it is that women are biologically programmed to reject men who are not high enough status. It also generates a prescription: become high status, and the biological drive will work in your favour. The evolutionary framing gives the claim scientific authority. And the pattern it points to is real, which is why it convinces: look around and many couples are ones in which he earns more. For some women it is also a reasonable choice: where a career runs into a ceiling, a partner who provides, as in the [[Tradwife]] and [[Provider]] model, offers security that work does not.

**The Friction:** What is contested is not the pattern but the drive. Read without the manosphere, the pattern says more about the labour market than about desire. Where a glass ceiling keeps women's careers and earnings lower, a partner who earns more is less a preference than one of the few routes to security and status left open. And the pattern is hard to break, because it feeds itself: even in Norway, husbands tend to rank higher among men's earners than their wives do among women's, and once a household organises itself around the higher earner, her earnings fall further behind (Almås et al., 2023). In the manosphere the same pattern becomes a claim about every woman: she is wired to want a man with more money and status than her own, and will trade up as soon as one comes along. Evolutionary psychology research on mate preferences is substantially more complex, contextually variable, and contested than that claim implies. The claim is also unfalsifiable as deployed: any relationship where a woman dates a man of higher status confirms it; any relationship where she dates a man of lower status is explained away. [[SMV (Sexual Market Value)]] is the market framework that makes **hypergamy** operational: if attractiveness is a score, **hypergamy** describes how women shop. [[Womanosphere]] adopts the same concept in a different register: women should leverage their hypergamic drive strategically. On TikTok the strategy became a product: in 2022 the hashtag had 126 million views, and dating coaches, [[Influencer|influencers]] who had turned it into a service, charged $150 a session to teach women how to marry wealthy. A sociologist at the University of Michigan called it "unlikely to be a terribly successful strategy", since women in the US now graduate from college at higher rates than men (*BuzzFeed News*, 2022). [[Incel]] ideology uses **hypergamy** to explain why men at the bottom of the SMV hierarchy are rejected: they are simply not high enough value to overcome female hypergamic selection.

**Why This Matters:** **Hypergamy** makes visible how scientific-sounding vocabulary can function as an unfalsifiable explanation for personal experience. Once the claim is biological and universal, no counter-evidence is possible, and the circumstances in which women actually choose drop out of view.
