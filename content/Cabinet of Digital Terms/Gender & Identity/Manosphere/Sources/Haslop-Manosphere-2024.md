## Haslop, Ringrose, Cambazoglu & Milne (2024) — Mainstreaming the Manosphere's Misogyny Through Affective Homosocial Currencies

**Haslop, C., Ringrose, J., Cambazoglu, I. & Milne, B. (2024).** Mainstreaming the Manosphere's Misogyny Through Affective Homosocial Currencies: Exploring How Teen Boys Navigate the Andrew Tate Effect. *Social Media + Society*. https://doi.org/10.1177/20563051241228811

### What this source contributes

In the summer of 2022 Andrew Tate became a concern for media, parents and school leaders as his content spread among young people, especially boys. The authors combine a discourse and content analysis of his videos with a small focus group of boys aged 13 and 14 in London. Tate's content mainstreams manosphere ideology; it plays on boys' fears about their economic futures and their place among men, while he presents himself as an authentic maverick offering hope through dating and business advice. These tropes serve his business model in the attention economy, and among the boys they work as shared currency, often read as humour. The authors advise critical digital literacy that helps young people understand such business models, rather than attacking the message, which plays into the maverick image.

### Analytical function in Manosphere

The source that brings the boys into the entry: what the content does among them, and what helps. It grounds the Friction's closing sentence and links the manosphere to the attention economy as a business.
