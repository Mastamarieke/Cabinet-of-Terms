## 1. Now comes the 'womanosphere': the anti-feminist media telling women to be thin, fertile and Republican

**Author:** The Guardian
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Journalism
**Publisher:** The Guardian (syndicated on AOL)
**URL:** https://www.aol.com/news/now-comes-womanosphere-anti-feminist-120004602.html

### What this source contributes

Describes "an organized effort to create an alternative rightwing media ecosystem targeting young female US audiences": Brett Cooper's YouTube channel, the magazines *Evie* and *The Conservateur*, Candace Owens's Club Candace, Alex Clark's *Culture Apothecary*, Allie Beth Stuckey's *Relatable* and Riley Gaines's *Gaines for Girls*. The source of the word's second meaning. Author and original Guardian URL still to be checked.

## 2. Young conservative women build an alternative to the manosphere

**Author:** Max Tani
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Journalism
**Publisher:** Semafor
**URL:** https://www.semafor.com/article/03/09/2025/young-conservative-women-build-an-alternative-to-the-manosphere

### What this source contributes

Reports (10 March 2025) on young conservative women building their own media: Brett Cooper, 1.4 million subscribers and 17 million views across her first ten episodes; Alex Clark and her "cuteservatives", with podcasts on microplastics, hormonal birth control and homeschooling; Riley Gaines. The article does not itself use the word "womanosphere". Source of game card 069.
