***Some people walk into a room and everyone wants to talk to them. Now there is a word for it, and a course to learn it.***

**Literal meaning:** **Rizz** is the ability to attract someone through charm and style; as a verb, *to rizz someone up* is to win them over. Oxford University Press, which made it Word of the Year in 2023, derives it from the middle of *charisma*, like *fridge* from *refrigerator*. The streamer who made it famous disagrees: "Rizz meant 'game'," Kai Cenat said. "People say it's short for charisma. But like, not to me." The two readings pull apart: charisma is something you have, game is something you practise.

**Origin:** Cenat, a Black Twitch streamer from the Bronx with more than eight million followers, says it started with him and his friends from back home; from his streams it spread across TikTok, and once it went viral there he stopped using it himself, saying TikTok had "butchered" the word. Use peaked in June 2023, when the actor Tom Holland said in an interview that he had "no rizz whatsoever". Oxford chose it over finalists including *Swiftie*, *situationship* and *parasocial*.

> Charm named as a skill — practised on stream and sold in courses, while the word still sounds like something you simply have.

**The Appeal:** **Rizz** names something real and widely wanted: the ease with which some people connect. For young people it is a word for what they see in others and want for themselves, and it is light: admitting, like Holland, that you have none is part of the joke.

**The Friction:** In Cenat's version, rizz was never effortless: it is game, a skill he talks through on his streams. Those streams are part of the [[Creator Economy]], where an audience is an income, and that makes rizz easy to sell. In [[SMV (Sexual Market Value)]] terms rizz becomes one more score, and the self-improvement market offers to raise it: [[Looksmaxxing]] works on the looks of face and body, rizz on how you come across, and [[Podcast-bro]] coaches script the lines.

**Why This Matters:** **Rizz** makes visible how fast a word from one group of friends becomes a dictionary entry and then a product. The dictionary heard charisma; the man who spread it meant game, and game is what the courses sell.
