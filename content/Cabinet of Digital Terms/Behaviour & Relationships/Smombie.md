***Walking while staring at a screen. A word that needed inventing because the behaviour needed naming.***

**Literal meaning:** Smombie — a portmanteau of *smartphone* and *zombie* — describes a person walking in public while absorbed in their phone, unaware of their surroundings. The term captures both the physical posture and the apparent absence of conscious engagement with the environment.

**Origin:** The term was coined in Germany around 2015 — *Smombie* won the Jugendwort (Youth Word) of the Year award in Germany in 2015 — and spread to other languages as the phenomenon it named became globally recognisable. Cities responded in the 2010s, a sign that the behaviour had crossed the threshold from personal habit to public problem. The pavement was there first: Chongqing marked a phone lane in September 2014, a year before *Smombie* became the Jugendwort, and Bodegraven laid lights into the pavement at a crossing in 2017 (see [[Wexting]]). Honolulu chose the law instead: since October 2017 its Distracted Walking Law fines pedestrians who look at a phone while crossing, from $15 for a first offence to $99 for repeat offenders, with a call to 911 as the only exception.

> A person physically present in public space while cognitively absent — attention captured by a screen while the body navigates the world on autopilot.

**The Appeal:** The phone is often serving a real purpose: navigation, communication, time management. Many smombie moments are functional, not mindless. The device is doing useful work and the user is managing two things simultaneously — a normal feature of urban life.

**The Friction:** The physical and cognitive cost is real. [[Tech Neck]] — cervical spine strain from sustained downward head posture — is the bodily consequence. [[Phubbing]] is the social consequence: the smombie is not only ignoring their surroundings but the people in them. [[Cognitive Surrender]] is the longer-term pattern: the habit of continuous phone engagement in public space erodes the capacity for unmediated presence. The word "zombie" is not accidental — it names the quality of movement without awareness, the uncanny gap between body and attention.

**Why This Matters:** Smombie names something that existed before the word but became visible once named. The person walking into traffic, the parent pushing a buggy while scrolling — these are not failures of character. They are the predictable outcome of a device designed to capture and hold attention, carried into every context of daily life.
