***The word has a history. Every time it has been used at scale, something worse followed.***

**Literal meaning:** **Subhuman** describes a person or group as less than fully human — beneath the threshold of humanity that commands moral consideration. The word and its equivalents (*untermenschen*, "animals," "vermin," "parasites") have a documented role in preparing populations for mass violence: they remove the psychological barriers against harming members of the target group.

**Origin:** The term has a documented history in genocidal rhetoric, most explicitly in Nazi ideology where *Untermenschen* was used to classify Jewish people, Roma, and others as racially inferior. Academic research by Gregory Stanton (genocide scholar), James Waller, and Ervin Staub has established dehumanising language as a predictable precursor in the sequence leading to mass atrocity. The term's circulation in contemporary digital discourse — applied to migrants, minorities, and political opponents — has been documented by researchers including Vidhya Ramalingam and organisations including the Global Network on Extremism and Technology. In November 2023 Donald Trump said he would "root out" political opponents who "live like vermin", words historians compared to those of Hitler and Mussolini ([ABC News, 2023](https://abcnews.com/Politics/trump-compares-political-opponents-vermin-root-alarming-historians/story?id=104847748)).

> Language that positions a person or group as less than human — with a documented historical role in preparing populations for violence.

**The Appeal:** From the perspective of those who use it: the term makes extreme political positions feel justified. If the target group is genuinely **subhuman**, then policies that would otherwise be unacceptable become proportionate responses. The dehumanisation is the permission structure.

**The Friction:** [[Dehumanization]] — Bandura's moral disengagement framework — describes the psychological mechanism: once a group is categorised as less than human, the inhibitions against harming them are systematically reduced. The research is not speculative — it is reconstructed from genocide perpetrators' own accounts. Digital platforms accelerate the circulation of dehumanising language by treating it as expression rather than as the precursor stage of a documented harm sequence. Predatory labels travel the same way: in the month after the Florida Senate passed the "Don't Say Gay" bill in March 2022, tweets linking LGBTQ people to [[Groomer|"groomer"]], "pedophile" or "predator" rose by 406 percent ([Yurcaba, 2022](https://www.nbcnews.com/nbc-out/out-news/-dont-say-gay-bill-passed-lgbtq-online-hate-surged-400-rcna42617)). [[Great Replacement]] — conspiracy theory framing migrants as existential threat — is the ideological context in which **subhuman** language currently circulates most actively. [[Deplatforming]] is the primary countermeasure; its effectiveness is contested.

**Why This Matters:** **Subhuman** is a word that functions as a stage in a sequence. Knowing the sequence — dehumanisation, persecution, mass violence — makes the word's presence in mainstream discourse a different kind of signal than general offensiveness.
