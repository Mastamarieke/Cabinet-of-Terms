## 2. Frictie: Ethiek in tijden van dataïsme

**Author:** Miriam Rasch
**Year:** 2020
**Type:** Book (essay)
**Publisher:** De Bezige Bij
**URL:** https://networkcultures.org/blog/2020/05/11/out-now-frictie-ethiek-in-tijden-van-dataisme-miriam-rasch/

### What this source contributes

The Dutch essay on dataism: the belief that everything can be translated into data, and that data pins the world down and makes it manageable. Rasch sets friction against the ideal of an automated world, and asks for whom and to what end.

## 5. The Philosophy of Data

**Author:** David Brooks
**Year:** 2013
**Type:** Journalism (column)
**Publisher:** The New York Times, 4 February
**URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/2013/02/05/opinion/brooks-the-philosophy-of-data.html

### What this source contributes

The column that introduced the word: "If you asked me to describe the rising philosophy of the day, I'd say it is Data-ism." Brooks expected data to reduce our prejudices and show patterns of behaviour no one had noticed. Used in the semantic landscape. The column is behind the New York Times paywall and could not be opened (11-10). The quotation and the date of 4 February 2013 are taken from two independent citations: Wikipedia's article *Dataism*, and a blog post of 20 March 2013 (infostory.com) that quotes the column six weeks after it appeared.

## 6. The UK exam debacle reminds us that algorithms can't fix broken systems

**Author:** Karen Hao
**Year:** 2020
**Type:** Journalism
**Publisher:** MIT Technology Review, 20 August
**URL:** https://www.technologyreview.com/2020/08/20/1007502/uk-exam-algorithm-cant-fix-broken-system/

The protest of 16 August 2020, when hundreds of students chanted "Fuck the algorithm" outside the Department for Education in London, and the analysis that the algorithm hurt students from working-class and disadvantaged communities and inflated the scores of students from private schools.
