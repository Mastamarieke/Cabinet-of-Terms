***Your data is worth money. Who do you think profits from it?***

**Literal meaning:** **[[Dataism]]** puts *-ism*, the ending of religions and schools of thought, after *data*, Latin for "things given", and so turns what is given into a creed. It names the belief that decisions based on data are better than human judgement, that more data is always better, and that collecting it is progress in itself.

**Origin:** ==Yuval Noah Harari made it the name of an emerging belief system== ([[Secondary-Dataism#1. Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow|Harari, 2016]]: Homo Deus, in which the flow of information becomes the highest value). ==José van Dijck called it a widespread secular belief, one that works because people trust their personal information to the platforms they use== ([[VanDijck-Dataism-2014|Van Dijck, 2014]]: data as the currency people pay with for their services, and the trust that keeps the trade going). In the Netherlands Miriam Rasch, a researcher at the Institute of Network Cultures, took up the word and described ==the belief that everything can be translated into data, and that data pins the world down and makes it manageable== ([[Secondary-Dataism#2. Frictie: Ethiek in tijden van dataïsme|Rasch, 2020]]: Frictie, her essay on ethics in times of dataism, which asks for whom and to what end).

**The Appeal:** **[[Dataism]]** rests on real insights. Decisions based on data can correct human bias, large data sets show patterns that no one would notice by looking, and in many situations more information is better than less. In 2006 ==Clive Humby==[^humby] put the promise in five words, *data is the new oil*: worth little raw, and a fortune once refined.

[^humby]: A British mathematician who, with Edwina Dunn, founded dunnhumby, the company behind Tesco's Clubcard, launched in 1995 as the first loyalty card of its kind. He coined the phrase in 2006. [Wikipedia: 'Clive Humby'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Humby)

**The Friction:** Whoever gets to decide what it says about you: **[[Dataism|dataism]]** hides the choices behind the numbers, such as which things are counted, whose lives are easiest to measure and which outcome is optimised. ==When exams in England were cancelled in 2020, an algorithm set students' grades partly from their school's past results, and close to 40 percent came out lower than teachers had predicted== ([[Secondary-Dataism#4. UK government makes dramatic exam results U-turn after national outcry|Reynolds, 2020]]: students at disadvantaged schools lost out more often). The government had handed a public decision to a calculation, which is [[Cybernetic Governance]], and the calculation carried the old gap between schools into each student's grade. ==Donna Haraway==[^haraway] described this in 1985 as the [[Informatics of Domination]], old hierarchies carried on in information systems. Because the number looked neutral, the harm looked like no one's doing, which is [[Algorithmic Violence]].

[[Surveillance Capitalism]], Shoshana Zuboff's term for selling predictions made from behavioural data, profits from the belief that more data is always better. The same belief makes the trade of [[Data Brokers]], companies that sell profiles of people who have never heard of them, look like an ordinary business.

[^haraway]: An American philosopher who brought information technology and feminist theory together. [Wikipedia: 'Donna Haraway'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Haraway)

**Why This Matters:** Knowing the term **[[Dataism|dataism]]** helps you see the belief that drives it: that your data knows you better than you do, which is what lets tech companies steer you, predict what you will do next and sell those predictions. Rasch sets friction against the ideal of an automated world, by not letting a number settle things by itself. ==After a national outcry, the English government dropped the algorithm's grades within a week== ([[Secondary-Dataism#4. UK government makes dramatic exam results U-turn after national outcry|Reynolds, 2020]]: the U-turn of August 2020). The next time a score decides for you, a grade, a credit rating, a match on an app or the rating of your face that [[Looksmaxxing]] forums promise, ask what was counted, by whom, and what was left out.
