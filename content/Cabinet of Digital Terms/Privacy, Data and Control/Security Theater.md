***You took off your shoes at the airport. Who was that for?***

**Literal meaning:** *Security* means being free from danger, from Latin *securus*, without care. A *theater* is a performance put on for an audience. In **security theater** the audience is the people who are supposed to be protected, and the performance is that they feel safe. The term names measures designed to be seen more than to stop anything. They calm people down and leave the risk where it was.

**Origin:** Bruce Schneier, a security technologist, coined the term in *Beyond Fear* (2003) for aviation security after 9/11. Passengers took off their shoes, took out their laptops and walked through body scanners. In his view these steps were highly visible and of little use against a determined attacker. Their main work was to make passengers feel protected and to let politicians be seen doing something.

> Security measures that are meant to be seen more than to protect, so that safety is performed without being provided.

**The Appeal:** After a failure, people want to see a response, and a visible measure gives them one whether or not it works. For an organisation, a measure it can point to protects it from blame and fines when something goes wrong.

**The Friction:** For whoever needed to be seen doing something. You do what the measure asks, and the risk stays where it was. Since July 2025 British platforms have had to check that their users are adults. ==Within days, gamers passed Discord's face scan with the face of a character from the video game Death Stranding== ([Gizmodo, 2025](https://gizmodo.com/death-stranding-is-helping-uk-users-bypass-age-verification-laws-2000635584): age checks under the Online Safety Act, while UK sign-ups at ProtonVPN rose by more than 1,400 percent). You hand over your face, a piece of [[Biometric Surveillance]], for a lock that a game character can open. ==In 2017 Bill Burr, who in 2003 wrote the American rules that demand capitals, digits and symbols in passwords, said he regretted much of what he did== ([Engadget, 2017](https://www.engadget.com/2017-08-08-nist-new-password-guidelines.html): Burr worked at NIST, the US standards institute). His rules had led to passwords that look strong and are easy to guess. With personal data the same move is called [[Privacy Washing]], where reassurance takes the place of protection.

**Why This Matters:** Schneier argues that **security theater** is not harmless. It takes money and attention away from measures that work, and people who feel safe become less careful. You meet it at every check you are asked to pass, from the age scan to the password rule and the queue at the airport. The question to ask is what the check actually stops, and who tested it. ==In the same year NIST, the standards institute Burr worked for, dropped compulsory symbols and regular password changes and advised long passphrases instead== ([Engadget, 2017](https://www.engadget.com/2017-08-08-nist-new-password-guidelines.html): the institute's revised guidelines).
