## Cirucci (2024) — Oversharing the super safe stuff: "Privacy-washing" in Apple iPhone and Google Pixel commercials

**Cirucci, A. M. (2024). Oversharing the super safe stuff: "Privacy-washing" in Apple iPhone and Google Pixel commercials.** *First Monday*. https://doi.org/10.5210/fm.v29i5.13321

---

### What this source contributes

A critical discourse analysis of two phone adverts, Apple's *Oversharing* for the iPhone and Google's *Super Safe Stuff Securer* for the Pixel, read next to each company's privacy policy. The adverts "conflate privacy with security, relying on powerful imageries that depict the phones as safes from which data cannot travel", while the policies treat privacy and security separately. In *Oversharing* people say their data out loud, and "at least half of the data spoken aloud … are actually implicit data — geolocation, search history, credit card details"; the advert ends with the Apple logo turning into a lock. "Obviously left out" are institutional privacy and inferred data: what the company and its partners collect and work out.

Cirucci defines privacy-washing as "the purposeful conflation of security with privacy, the disregarding of more granular definitions of privacy … and a general reliance on offline privacy expectations that are no longer applicable to online spaces." She notes that the term had "shown up briefly in some Californian and Canadian law documents" before it reached academic analysis, and proposes adopting it to build literacy about types of data.

### Analytical function in Privacy Washing

The mechanism, and the image. Cirucci shows how the promise works: the advert stages a stranger who overhears, so that the flow of data to the company stays out of the picture. The lock at the end of the advert is the sign the entry's landscape follows. She also gives the word's earliest traces (Origin).
