***The app says your privacy matters. How would you know if it didn't?***

**Literal meaning:** *Privacy* is the right to keep part of your life to yourself. *Washing* comes from *whitewashing*, painting over a stain so that the wall looks clean while the stain stays underneath. *Greenwashing* carried the word over to environmental claims, and **privacy washing** carries it over to personal data. It names a company that talks about protecting your privacy instead of protecting it. Its statements, settings, badges and adverts promise more than the practice behind them.

**Origin:** The term borrows the logic of [[Greenwashing|greenwashing]], environmental claims that go further than environmental practice. It gained ground as corporate privacy promises were followed, one after another, by documented breaches. Facebook gave assurances about user data before the Cambridge Analytica revelations in 2018. Google offered settings that kept recording where people went after they had switched them off.

> Talking about privacy instead of protecting it, with reassurance in place of the practice it describes.

**The Appeal:** Privacy sells. People prefer products that seem to protect them, regulators want to see compliance, and a reassuring sentence costs far less than changing how data is collected. The statements are rarely false in a narrow sense. They are written to be heard as meaning more than they say, the way Mark Zuckerberg's [["We have never and will never sell user data."]] was.

**The Friction:** You wouldn't. You are told your data is safe, and you have no way to check. ==In January 2019 Apple put up a billboard in Las Vegas that read What happens on your iPhone, stays on your iPhone== ([Seattle Times, 2019](https://www.seattletimes.com/business/apple-burns-google-in-giant-billboard-touting-privacy-at-ces/): next to the CES technology fair, aimed at Google). ==That summer contractors working for Apple turned out to be listening to fragments of Siri recordings, including conversations recorded by accident== ([Hern, 2019](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2019/jul/26/apple-contractors-regularly-hear-confidential-details-on-siri-recordings): The Guardian, based on one of the contractors). ==In 2025 Apple paid 95 million dollars to settle the case without admitting wrongdoing== ([NPR, 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/01/03/g-s1-40940/apple-settle-lawsuit-siri-privacy): class action over recordings made without Hey Siri). The promise works because the person reading it cannot see where the data goes. It protects a business, [[Surveillance Capitalism]], which sells predictions made from what people do. In the interface [[Dark Patterns]] do the same work, with a consent banner that looks like a choice and is designed for yes.

**Why This Matters:** Once you know the move, a privacy promise becomes a claim you can test. You meet it in the sentence itself, in *we never sell your data* or *your privacy matters to us*. The question then is what the sentence rules out, and who still gets the data without it being sold. [[Data Brokers]] trade profiles that companies say they do not sell. ==After the reporting Apple apologised, stopped keeping Siri recordings by default and let only users who opt in be listened to== ([CNBC, 2019](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/08/28/apple-apologizes-for-listening-to-siri-conversations.html): announcement of 28 August 2019).
