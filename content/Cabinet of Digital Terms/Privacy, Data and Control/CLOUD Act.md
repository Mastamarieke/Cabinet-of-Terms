***Your data is stored in Europe. Whose law decides who can read it?***

**Literal meaning:** **CLOUD Act** is short for *Clarifying Lawful Overseas Use of Data Act*. An *act* is a law passed by a parliament, here the US Congress, and the long name says what this one does. It makes clear (*clarifying*) when American authorities may legally (*lawful*) use data that is stored abroad (*overseas*). The short name was chosen so that its letters spell *cloud*, the everyday word for data kept on someone else's servers, which sounds weightless and everywhere. In practice the cloud is a server in a building, owned by a company in a particular country, and the law makes clear that this country decides. Since 2018 it has obliged American technology companies to hand over data on a US warrant, wherever in the world the server stands.

**Origin:** The law answered the *Microsoft Ireland* case, in which US prosecutors demanded emails that Microsoft kept on servers in Dublin, and Microsoft refused, arguing that a US warrant stops at the border. Congress passed the **CLOUD Act** in March 2018 while the case was before the Supreme Court, and the case was dropped.

> A US law that makes American cloud companies hand over data to US authorities, wherever the data is stored.

**The Appeal:** For investigators the law solves a real problem. Evidence of crimes is often stored on servers abroad, and until 2018 a warrant stopped where the server did. The law also lets other countries make agreements with the United States to reach the same evidence.

**The Friction:** The law of the country where the owner of the server is based, not where the server stands. In June 2025 the director of legal affairs of Microsoft France was asked under oath in the French Senate whether he could guarantee that French data would never reach US authorities without France's consent. ==He said he could not, adding that it had never happened yet== ([Sénat, 2025](https://www.senat.fr/compte-rendu-commissions/20250609/ce_commande_publique.html): hearing of 10 June 2025 on public procurement). That collides with the GDPR, the European privacy law that the [[Brussels Effect]] carries far beyond Europe. A European school, hospital or ministry that works with Microsoft, Amazon or Google keeps its data in Europe and still falls under a US warrant. Leaving is hard when the email, files and accounts of a whole organisation are built on one supplier, a form of [[Vendor Lock-in]]. States that depend on such suppliers end up renting the systems they govern with, which is what [[Sovereignty as a Service]] describes.

**Why This Matters:** *Stored in Europe* is the answer most people are given. The **CLOUD Act** turns it into half an answer, for the school or work account you were given as much as for a ministry. The question to put to your school or employer is who owns the system they chose, and what happens if a US warrant arrives. ==Since 2022 France certifies cloud services as trusted only if they are subject to European law alone, with limits on non-European ownership== ([Wikipedia, SecNumCloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SecNumCloud): version 3.2 of the French government's security label).
