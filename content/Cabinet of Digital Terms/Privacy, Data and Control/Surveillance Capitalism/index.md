Many of them meet here over something handed over: a photo, a location, a name that should have been forgotten (**Deadnaming**).

  Follow one location. In 2016 a player of Pokémon GO walks to a park to catch a creature, and the game records where; in 2024 Niantic said that scans made by players were training a model of the physical world. A location from another app, Candy Crush or Tinder, reaches **Data Brokers** such as Gravy Analytics, who sell to companies and to governments. On the way it passes **Microtargeting**, where it decides which advert one person sees, and the **Panopticon**, where people act as if they are watched. ***Your behaviour is the raw material. Your prediction is the product.***

**Literal meaning:** *Surveillance* is French for watching over, from *sur*, over, and *veiller*, to keep watch, a word of police, armies and spies. *Capitalism* is an economy built on private ownership and profit. Joined, the watching is done for profit. **Surveillance capitalism** is ==Shoshana Zuboff==[^zuboff]'s term for an economy that takes everything people leave behind online, every click, pause and scroll, as raw material, turns it into predictions of what they will do next, and sells those predictions. Meta describes its own feed in these terms, saying that [its AI systems "predict how valuable a piece of content might be to you"](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/06/how-ai-ranks-content-on-facebook-and-instagram/ "Read how Meta explains the AI that ranks your feed (Clegg, 2023)."), with predictions "based on behavior". Zuboff's key term is *behavioural surplus*, the data collected beyond what a service needs and kept because it predicts.

[^zuboff]: An American social psychologist and professor emerita at Harvard Business School. She first named surveillance capitalism in an academic article in 2015, and worked it out in a book in 2019. [Wikipedia: 'Shoshana Zuboff'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshana_Zuboff)

**Origin:** In 1988 Zuboff had described the computer-monitored workplace as an information [[Panopticon]], where workers cannot tell when they are being watched. In 2015 she named the new power *Big Other*, an echo of Big Brother in [[1984]] spread across a network of computers instead of held by one watcher ([[Zuboff-SurveillanceCapitalism-2015|Zuboff, 2015]]: her first academic paper on the term, built on two articles by Google's chief economist). The full argument followed in *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* (2019), which traces it to ==Google's decision in 2000–2001 to use search data to target adverts, first on the side, then as its core business== ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#2. The Age of Surveillance Capitalism|Zuboff, 2019]]: the book).

> The economic logic in which human experience is claimed as raw material, turned into predictions and sold, as Shoshana Zuboff named and analysed it in 2019.

**The Appeal:** **Surveillance capitalism** pays for services people use every day, for free. The trade is real, with search, maps and messages offered in exchange for data, and it is the same bargain as the [[Attention Economy]], paid in data instead of attention.

**The Friction:** The trade is never put to you. Zuboff's point is that it is not disclosed, not negotiated and not really agreed, or as she put it, =="Our ignorance is their bliss"== ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#3. Zó steelt en verkoopt Big Tech jouw gegevens|VPRO Tegenlicht, 2024]]: interview with Zuboff). Ask what happens to your data and you get reassurance instead of protection, which is [[Privacy Washing]]. ==Google kept recording people's movements after they had switched off Location History== ([Korn, 2022](https://www.cnn.com/2022/11/14/tech/google-location-tracking-settlement): a $391.5 million settlement with 40 US states). Mark Zuckerberg's line [["We have never and will never sell user data."|"we have never and will never sell user data"]] is true in a narrow sense, because Meta does not sell the data but sells access to the people the data describes.

The data does not stay where you gave it either, because ==most apps pass it on to companies you never chose== ([[Binns-SurveillanceCapitalism-2018|Binns et al., 2018]]: 959,000 apps in the US and UK, children's apps among the worst). Nothing had to be hacked for ==Cambridge Analytica==[^ca] ==to reach the data of up to 87 million people through a quiz== ([Time, 2018](https://time.com/5205314/facebook-cambridge-analytica-breach/): about 300,000 people installed it). A government can buy such data, and it can also take over the infrastructure. In February 2025 Elon Musk's ==DOGE==[^doge] team ==gained access to the Treasury's payment system== ([Ingram, 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/elon-musk-doge-usaid-treasury-government-rcna190450)), the merger of private infrastructure and public power that [[Muskism]] names.

[^doge]: The Department of Government Efficiency, set up by executive order on 20 January 2025, at the start of Donald Trump's second term, with Elon Musk as its de facto leader. [Wikipedia: 'Department of Government Efficiency'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency)
[^ca]: A British political consulting firm that built profiles of voters from Facebook data to target them with political adverts, for Donald Trump's 2016 campaign among others. It closed in May 2018. [Wikipedia: 'Cambridge Analytica'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Analytica)

Leaving costs more than staying once your photos and contacts sit with one company, which is [[Vendor Lock-in]]. A company that knows you will stay can make the service worse, what Cory Doctorow calls [[Enshittification]]. The prediction can also set your price, and ==the same dishwasher can cost you hundreds of dollars more or less than your neighbour== ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#19. Historicus en journalist Jill Lepore: ‘Als je geen datacenter voor je deur wilt, stop dan met alles aan AI te vragen’|Van der Steen, 2026]]: the historian Jill Lepore on surveillance pricing). What makes all this look normal is [[Dataism]], the belief that data is neutral and that more of it is always better. Roxane van Iperen calls the result environment design, in which people are not ordered but steered by the space built around them ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#4. Big Tech maakt van burgers vee|Van Iperen, 2026]]). China's [[Social Credit System]] is held up as the opposite, a state that gives orders, and ==its blacklists stopped people buying plane tickets 17.5 million times in 2018== ([Jee, 2019](https://www.technologyreview.com/2019/03/04/136791/chinas-social-credit-system-stopped-millions-of-people-buying-travel-tickets/)). In the West insurers, employers and platforms score behaviour too, only without a central list.

Meta's camera glasses, promoted by the [[Influencer]] Kylie Jenner ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#13. Met getuite lippen promoot Kylie Jenner haar Meta AI-bril. Een mode-item of surveillance-kapitalisme?|Hoogenraad, 2026]]), film the people around the wearer, and ==two Swedish newspapers reported==[^swedish] that wearers' private video goes to workers in Kenya who train the glasses' AI. That is [[Ghost Work]], hidden labour, done on footage of people who never agreed to be filmed. When Roel Maalderink and Bits of Freedom ==mocked the glasses in a video==[^video], Meta took it off Instagram, an act of [[Deplatforming]] by the maker of the product being criticised.

[^swedish]: Svenska Dagbladet and Göteborgs-Posten interviewed 32 employees of Sama, Meta's subcontractor in Nairobi; nine of them label video, images and speech for Meta's AI. Published 25 February 2026. [[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#20. Sex, toabesök & våld: Så hamnar Metas data på skärm i Kenya|Göteborgs-Posten: 'Sex, toabesök & våld: Så hamnar Metas data på skärm i Kenya']]
[^video]: The programme maker Roel Maalderink filmed Meta employees outside the company's Amsterdam office with the glasses, together with the Dutch digital rights organisation Bits of Freedom. Meta removed the video for harassment and intimidation; Bits of Freedom called it censorship. [[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#16. Meta haalt satirische video Roel Maalderink over camerabril offline|NOS: 'Meta haalt satirische video Roel Maalderink over camerabril offline']]

**Why This Matters:** Once you see human experience as raw material, [[Recommender Systems|recommender systems]], [[Dark Patterns|dark patterns]] and dopamine loops look like parts of one system. The director Marc Silver made a film with Zuboff about where it can end, *Molly vs the Machines*[^film]. ==Molly Russell, fourteen, was flooded with posts about depression and self-harm on Instagram before she took her own life== ([NPO Doc, 2026](https://www.npodoc.nl/artikelen/interview-marc-silver): interview with the director; [Molly Rose Foundation, 2025](https://mollyrosefoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/proof3_PervasivebyDesign.pdf): eight years later, test accounts of a fifteen-year-old girl were still recommended harmful posts in 97% of Instagram Reels, with an advert from a well-known brand about every tenth video). Silver asks parents whether they would send their child to the bedroom with a stranger who watches them and steers what they see. [[Ethical Design]], design that puts users' wellbeing and autonomy above engagement and extraction, asks the makers whether their design is allowed to cost the company something. In August 2026 Meta settled with 51 US ==attorneys general==[^ag] and [agreed to a two-hour daily limit for under-18s, no access between midnight and six, and a feed without the algorithm for those who choose it](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-secures-171-billion-and-groundbreaking-reforms-meta "Read the New York Attorney General's announcement of the settlement (August 2026)."). By then ==a court in New Mexico==[^nm] had ordered it to pay $942 million for harming children's mental health.

[^ag]: The chief legal adviser and chief law enforcement officer of a US state. [Wikipedia: 'State attorney general'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_attorney_general)
[^film]: The documentary uses the coroner's inquest into Molly Russell's death to show how surveillance capitalism rose to power over two decades, and what that means for democracy. Shown in the Netherlands by VPRO as *Molly versus het algoritme*. [PA: 'Molly Russell would be fighting to make online world safe, bereaved father says'](https://www.kentonline.co.uk/news/national/molly-russell-would-be-fighting-to-make-online-world-safe-bereaved-father-says-167181/)
[^nm]: In New Mexico a jury ordered Meta to pay $375 million in March 2026 and a judge added $567 million in August, for harming children's mental health. Meta appeals.
