Many of them meet here over something handed over: a photo, a location, a name that should have been forgotten (**Deadnaming**).

  Follow one location. In 2016 a player of Pokémon GO walks to a park to catch a creature, and the game records where; in 2024 Niantic said that scans made by players were training a model of the physical world. A location from another app, Candy Crush or Tinder, reaches **Data Brokers** such as Gravy Analytics, who sell to companies and to governments. On the way it passes **Microtargeting**, where it decides which advert one person sees, and the **Panopticon**, where people act as if they are watched. A government that buys can also be entered from inside: in 2025 Elon Musk's DOGE team reached the Treasury's payment system (**Muskism**), and the acting director of ICE wanted deportation to run like Prime.

  Other neighbours try to hand over less. **Obfuscation**, the **Fediverse** and **Privacy by Design** each offer a way, and the **Brussels Effect** makes companies elsewhere answer to European rules. More and more, people take the companies to court.

***Your behaviour is the raw material. Your prediction is the product.***

**Literal meaning:** *Surveillance* is French for watching over (*sur*, over, and *veiller*, to keep watch), a word of police, armies and spies; *capitalism* is an economy built on private ownership and profit. Joined, the watching is done for profit. **Surveillance capitalism** is **Shoshana Zuboff**'s term for an economy that takes everything people leave behind online, every click, pause and scroll, as raw material, turns it into predictions of what they will do next, and sells those predictions. Meta describes its own feed in these terms: [its AI systems "predict how valuable a piece of content might be to you"](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/06/how-ai-ranks-content-on-facebook-and-instagram/ "Read how Meta explains the AI that ranks your feed (Clegg, 2023)."), with predictions "based on behavior". Advertisers and political campaigns buy them to reach one person at a predicted weak spot: [[Microtargeting]]. Its key term is *behavioural surplus*: data collected beyond what a service needs, kept because it predicts. [[BUMMER]][^bummer], an acronym by ==Jaron Lanier==[^lanier], names the same business model from inside the industry.

[^bummer]: Behaviours of Users Modified and Made into an Empire for Rent: Lanier's name for the social media business model, which modifies user behaviour at scale and rents that influence to anyone who pays. From his book *Ten Arguments for Deleting Your Social Media Accounts Right Now* (2018).

[^lanier]: An American computer scientist, considered one of the founders of virtual reality. [Wikipedia: 'Jaron Lanier'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaron_Lanier)

**Origin:** In 1988 Zuboff had described the computer-monitored workplace as an information [[Panopticon]], where workers cannot tell when they are being watched. In 2015 she named the new power *Big Other*: an echo of Big Brother in [[1984]], spread across a network of computers instead of held by one watcher ([[Zuboff-SurveillanceCapitalism-2015|Zuboff, 2015]]: her first academic paper on the term, built on two articles by Google's chief economist). The full argument followed in *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* (2019), with a founding moment: ==Google's decision in 2000–2001 to use search data to target adverts, first on the side, then as its core business== ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#2. The Age of Surveillance Capitalism|Zuboff, 2019]]: the book).

> The economic logic in which human behavioural experience is claimed as raw material, processed into prediction products, and sold; named and analysed by **Shoshana Zuboff** in 2019.

**The Appeal:** **Surveillance capitalism** pays for services people use every day, for free. The trade is real: search, maps and messages in exchange for data. It is the same bargain as the [[Attention Economy]], paid in data instead of attention.

**The Friction:** The trade is never put to you. Zuboff's point is that it is not disclosed, not negotiated and not really agreed: =="Our ignorance is their bliss"== ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#3. Zó steelt en verkoopt Big Tech jouw gegevens|VPRO Tegenlicht, 2024]]: interview with Zuboff). Ask what happens to your data and you get reassurance instead of protection, which is [[Privacy Washing]]: ==Google kept recording people's movements after they had switched off Location History== ([Korn, 2022](https://www.cnn.com/2022/11/14/tech/google-location-tracking-settlement): a $391.5 million settlement with 40 US states). Mark Zuckerberg's line [["We have never and will never sell user data."|"we have never and will never sell user data"]] is true in a narrow sense: Meta does not sell the data, it sells access to the people the data describes. In Europe the trade has since been put to people, with a price: since November 2023 users of Facebook and Instagram can pay to stop seeing adverts, and while they pay, ==their information will not be used for ads== ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#21. Facebook and Instagram to Offer Subscription for No Ads in Europe|Meta, 2023]]: €9.99 a month on the web at first, cut by 40 percent a year later). Not being tracked became the paid version: [[Privacy as a Premium]].

And the data does not stay where you gave it. ==Most apps pass it on to companies you never chose== ([[Binns-SurveillanceCapitalism-2018|Binns et al., 2018]]: 959,000 apps in the US and UK, children's apps among the worst), many of them outside the EU, which is why European rules try to reach them wherever they are: the [[Brussels Effect]]. Nothing had to be hacked for ==Cambridge Analytica==[^ca] ==to reach the data of up to 87 million people through a quiz== ([Time, 2018](https://time.com/5205314/facebook-cambridge-analytica-breach/): about 300,000 people installed it), and a hack of ==Gravy Analytics showed locations taken from thousands of ordinary apps== ([Cox, 2025](https://www.404media.co/candy-crush-tinder-myfitnesspal-see-the-thousands-of-apps-hijacked-to-spy-on-your-location/)), one of the largest [[Data Brokers]]. A government can buy, and it can become the next owner: in February 2025 Elon Musk's ==DOGE==[^doge] team ==gained access to the Treasury's payment system== ([Ingram, 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/elon-musk-doge-usaid-treasury-government-rcna190450)), the merger of private infrastructure and public power that [[Muskism]] names, and the acting director of ==ICE==[^ice] wanted deportation to run [["Like Prime, but with human beings."|"like Prime, but with human beings"]] ([MacDonald-Evoy, 2025](https://azmirror.com/2025/04/08/ice-director-envisions-amazon-like-mass-deportation-system-prime-but-with-human-beings/)). The same logic buys political power: [[Technofeudalism]], Yanis Varoufakis's word for platform owners who collect rent from users and businesses with no other option, and [[Broligarchy]], the tech owners who turned that success into political influence.

[^ice]: United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement, the federal agency that enforces immigration law. Its acting director from 2025 was Todd Lyons. [Wikipedia: 'U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement)

[^doge]: The Department of Government Efficiency, set up by executive order on 20 January 2025, at the start of Donald Trump's second term, with Elon Musk as its de facto leader. [Wikipedia: 'Department of Government Efficiency'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency)

[^ca]: A British political consulting firm that built profiles of voters from Facebook data to target them with political adverts, for Donald Trump's 2016 campaign among others. It closed in May 2018. [Wikipedia: 'Cambridge Analytica'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Analytica)

Leaving costs more than staying once your photos and contacts sit with one company: [[Vendor Lock-in]]. A company that knows you will stay can make the service worse, [[Enshittification]] in Cory Doctorow's word: ==Amazon put adverts into Prime Video for people who already paid== ([Rizzo, 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/09/22/amazon-prime-video-ads-start-in-2024.html): $2.99 a month to remove them). The prediction can also set your price: ==the same dishwasher can cost you hundreds of dollars more or less than your neighbour== ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#19. Historicus en journalist Jill Lepore: ‘Als je geen datacenter voor je deur wilt, stop dan met alles aan AI te vragen’|Van der Steen, 2026]] · [[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#20. Sex, toabesök & våld: Så hamnar Metas data på skärm i Kenya|SvD & GP (2026)]] · [[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#21. Facebook and Instagram to Offer Subscription for No Ads in Europe|Meta (2023)]]: the historian Jill Lepore on surveillance pricing). Predictions built on an unequal past repeat it at scale, [[Algorithmic Violence]], and what makes it look normal is [[Dataism]], the belief that data is neutral. Roxane van Iperen calls the result environment design: people are not ordered, they are steered by the space built around them ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#4. Big Tech maakt van burgers vee|Van Iperen, 2026]]). China's [[Social Credit System]] is held up as the opposite, a state that orders: ==its blacklists stopped people buying plane tickets 17.5 million times in 2018== ([Jee, 2019](https://www.technologyreview.com/2019/03/04/136791/chinas-social-credit-system-stopped-millions-of-people-buying-travel-tickets/)). In the West insurers, employers and platforms score behaviour too, without a central list.

Meta's camera glasses, promoted by the [[Influencer]] Kylie Jenner ([[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#13. Met getuite lippen promoot Kylie Jenner haar Meta AI-bril. Een mode-item of surveillance-kapitalisme?|Hoogenraad, 2026]]), film the people around the wearer, and ==two Swedish newspapers reported==[^swedish] that wearers' private video goes to workers in Kenya who train the glasses' AI: [[Ghost Work]], done on footage of people who never agreed to be filmed. When Roel Maalderink and Bits of Freedom ==mocked the glasses in a video==[^video], Meta took it off Instagram: [[Deplatforming]], by the maker of the product being criticised. Face recognition and filming demonstrators are in [[Biometric Surveillance]].

[^swedish]: Svenska Dagbladet and Göteborgs-Posten interviewed 32 employees of Sama, Meta's subcontractor in Nairobi; nine of them label video, images and speech for Meta's AI. Published 25 February 2026. [[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#20. Sex, toabesök & våld: Så hamnar Metas data på skärm i Kenya|Göteborgs-Posten: 'Sex, toabesök & våld: Så hamnar Metas data på skärm i Kenya']]

[^video]: The programme maker Roel Maalderink filmed Meta employees outside the company's Amsterdam office with the glasses, together with the Dutch digital rights organisation Bits of Freedom. Meta removed the video for harassment and intimidation; Bits of Freedom called it censorship. [[Secondary-SurveillanceCapitalism#16. Meta haalt satirische video Roel Maalderink over camerabril offline|NOS: 'Meta haalt satirische video Roel Maalderink over camerabril offline']]

**Why This Matters:** Once you see human experience as raw material, [[Recommender Systems|recommender systems]], [[Dark Patterns|dark patterns]] and dopamine loops look like parts of one system. The director Marc Silver made a film about where it can end, written with Zuboff: *Molly vs the Machines*[^film]. ==Molly Russell, fourteen, was flooded with posts about depression and self-harm on Instagram before she took her own life== ([NPO Doc, 2026](https://www.npodoc.nl/artikelen/interview-marc-silver): interview with the director; [Molly Rose Foundation, 2025](https://mollyrosefoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/proof3_PervasivebyDesign.pdf): eight years later, test accounts of a fifteen-year-old girl were still recommended harmful posts in 97% of Instagram Reels, with an advert from a well-known brand about every tenth video). Silver puts the question to parents: would you send your child to their bedroom with a stranger who watches them and steers what they see? [[Ethical Design]], design that puts users' wellbeing and autonomy above engagement and extraction, asks the makers the matching question: does the design cost the company something? Molly's father, Ian Russell, asks for the rule that already holds offline: ["We wouldn't let a car on our roads in the UK without it passing our safety standards"](https://www.kentonline.co.uk/news/national/molly-russell-would-be-fighting-to-make-online-world-safe-bereaved-father-says-167181/ "Read Ian Russell on applying offline rules to the online world (PA, 2026)."). Pushing back has started to work: ==in New Mexico a jury and a judge ordered Meta to pay $942 million for harming children's mental health== ([PBS NewsHour, 2026](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/new-mexico-court-orders-meta-to-pay-567-million-over-mental-health-harms-to-kids-online): $375 million from the jury in March, $567 million from the judge in August, Meta appeals), and in August 2026, settling with 51 US ==attorneys general==[^ag], [Meta agreed to a two-hour daily limit for under-18s, no access between midnight and six, and a feed without the algorithm for those who choose it](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-secures-171-billion-and-groundbreaking-reforms-meta "Read the New York Attorney General's announcement of the settlement (August 2026).").

[^ag]: The chief legal adviser and chief law enforcement officer of a US state. [Wikipedia: 'State attorney general'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_attorney_general)
