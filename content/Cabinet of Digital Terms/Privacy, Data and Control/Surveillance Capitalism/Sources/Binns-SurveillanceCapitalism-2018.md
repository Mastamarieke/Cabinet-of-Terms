## Binns et al. (2018) — Third Party Tracking in the Mobile Ecosystem

**Binns, R., Lyngs, U., Van Kleek, M., Zhao, J., Libert, T. & Shadbolt, N. (2018).** Third Party Tracking in the Mobile Ecosystem. *Proceedings of the 10th ACM Conference on Web Science*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3201064.3201089 · open version: https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.03603

---

### What this source contributes

An empirical study of third-party trackers in 959,000 apps from the US and UK Google Play stores. Third-party tracking lets companies identify users and follow their behaviour across many digital services. The study finds that most apps contain such tracking, and that its distribution is long-tailed: a few highly dominant trackers account for a large share of the coverage. Tracking differs between categories of apps; news apps and apps aimed at children are among the worst in the number of trackers. Tracking is also highly transnational, with many trackers operating outside the EU, which the authors see as a serious legal compliance challenge for the tracking industry.

### Analytical function in Surveillance Capitalism

Where Zuboff describes the logic, this study measures the plumbing. It answers the Friction question of where the data goes once it is given: not to the app alone, but to a small number of companies that see across most of the apps people use. Its finding that many trackers sit outside the EU explains why European rules have to reach companies elsewhere, which links the entry to the Brussels Effect.
