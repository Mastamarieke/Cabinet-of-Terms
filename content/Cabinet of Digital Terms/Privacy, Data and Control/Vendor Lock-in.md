***You can leave any time. So why haven't you?***

**Literal meaning:** A *vendor* is a seller, from French *vendre*, to sell. To be *locked in* is to be shut inside a room you cannot leave. Together the words describe a customer who walked in freely and can no longer walk out. **Vendor lock-in** is dependence on one supplier of technology. Switching has become so costly, in money, technology, contracts or plain effort, that leaving is no longer a real option, even when the supplier's interests drift away from yours.

**Origin:** The term comes from business computing. Organisations that built their work on one supplier's systems, such as SAP, Oracle or Salesforce, found that their data formats and connections made moving elsewhere too costly. Governments, hospitals and schools that handed core tasks to a technology company met the same problem. Ending the contract meant stopping the work.

> Dependence on one technology supplier so deep that leaving costs more than staying, whatever staying costs.

**The Appeal:** It starts as a good deal. The supplier knows the technology, works at scale and often does the job better than you could yourself. The lock grows slowly, with every file, account and habit that moves in.

**The Friction:** Because leaving costs more than staying, so you stay, even when it gets worse. ==In its 2024 case against Apple, the US Department of Justice argued that green message bubbles from Android phones keep teenagers on the iPhone, which 85 percent of them use== ([TechCrunch, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/21/doj-claims-green-bubbles-are-an-issue-in-iphone-monopoly-suit/): the antitrust suit of March 2024, quoting Apple's own emails). Cory Doctorow, the writer who coined [[Enshittification]], describes how platforms are good to their users at first and, once those users cannot leave, make the service worse to earn more from them. A school or ministry that is locked into American cloud services is also bound by an American law, the [[CLOUD Act]], which lets US authorities demand its data. ==Since March 2024 iPhone users in the EU can install apps from other app stores, because the Digital Markets Act, a European law for the largest platforms, requires it== ([Dotson, 2024](https://siliconangle.com/2024/03/06/apple-releases-ios-17-4-bringing-alternative-marketplaces-eu-upgrades/): iOS 17.4), one of the European rules behind the [[Brussels Effect]].

**Why This Matters:** The lock is built while you are not looking, one photo, contact and habit at a time. That makes **vendor lock-in** easiest to see at the start, when leaving is still free, at the moment you sign up or when your school or employer picks a system. The question then is what it would cost to leave in five years, and whether you can take your data with you. On the [[Fediverse]], a network of independently run servers joined by an open protocol, you can move your account to another server and take your followers along. ==The library of the University of Groningen left X in 2023 for the Mastodon server that SURF runs for Dutch education and research== ([University of Groningen, 2023](https://www.rug.nl/news/2023/10/231019-ub-groningen-quits-twitter): the library's announcement).

**Related terms:** [[Technofeudalism]] (platform owners as landlords: the locked-in user is a tenant who cannot take what they built along) · [[Sovereignty as a Service]] (states that rent the systems they govern with: lock-in at the scale of a country) · [[Surveillance Capitalism]] (selling predictions made from behavioural data: the longer you stay, the more of you there is to sell) · [[Dark Patterns]] (interfaces designed to mislead: a cancel button hidden deep in the settings is a lock made of design) · [[Deceptive Design]] (the legal name for interfaces that mislead, including ones that make leaving hard) · [[Fair Patterns]] (honest interface design: leaving made as easy as joining)
