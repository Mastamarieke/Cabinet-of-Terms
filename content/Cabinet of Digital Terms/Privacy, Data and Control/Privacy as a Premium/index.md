***The paid version has no ads. The free version watches everything you do. Who can afford not to be watched?***

**Literal meaning:** *Privacy* comes from *private*, Latin for set apart, and means the freedom to keep your personal life, and information about it, to yourself. A *premium*, from Latin *praemium*, a prize, is the paid tier above the free one. **[[Privacy as a Premium|Privacy as a premium]]** puts not being tracked in that paid tier, while the free version is paid for with data about what you do.

**Origin:** In 2010 Zizi Papacharissi already called privacy *a luxury commodity*, because ==free services are paid for with personal data, and protecting it takes knowledge that follows income and education== ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium#1. Privacy as a luxury commodity|Papacharissi, 2010]]: the essay that first called privacy a luxury commodity). In November 2023 that luxury got a price, when [Meta offered Facebook and Instagram without ads for €9.99 a month](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/10/facebook-and-instagram-to-offer-subscription-for-no-ads-in-europe/ "Read Meta's announcement, with the prices.") after [the EU Court of Justice had allowed an alternative "for an appropriate fee"](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0252 "Read the ruling of the EU Court of Justice (paragraph 150).").

> Being watched is the default and not being watched is the upgrade, so how much privacy you get depends on what you can afford.

**The Appeal:** Meta presented the choice as fair, saying that an internet paid for by adverts ["gives people access to personalised products and services regardless of their economic status"](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/10/facebook-and-instagram-to-offer-subscription-for-no-ads-in-europe/ "Read the quote in Meta's announcement."). That is true, since the free version is open to anyone with a phone. Meta also has a reason of its own. ==It earns substantially all of its revenue from advertising, and to its investors it calls adverts that use less of your data "less relevant and effective than our premium ad offerings"== ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium#10. Meta Platforms, Inc., Annual Report (Form 10-K) for the year ended 31 December 2025|Meta, 2026]]: annual report to US investors). In Meta's own words, the premium is the tracked advert.

**The Friction:** Those who can pay, so privacy becomes a class privilege, and not only through money. Refusing to be tracked costs one click, yet ==people with less formal education refuse far less often than people with more== ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium#9. Privacy and protection of personal data (2020 onwards), table isoc_cisci_prv20|Eurostat, 2025]]: EU survey of internet users, in 2025 71% of Europeans online with a higher education had refused their data for adverts, against 47% with little formal education, in the Netherlands 76% against 60%). The [[Digital Divide]], which once decided who could get online, now decides who finds the reject button. [[Dark Patterns]] in the question you must answer before you can go on make that harder, and ==hiding the button raised consent by more than twenty points== ([[Nouwens-PrivacyAsAPremium-2020|Nouwens et al., 2020]]: a study of consent pop-ups on 10,000 UK websites). Paying is no guarantee either, because ==on a third of European websites with a pay-or-track wall, one or two trackers kept running for those who paid== ([[MullerTribbensee-PrivacyAsAPremium-2024|Müller-Tribbensee, 2024]]: a study of 341 European websites with a pay-or-track wall), which makes the promise a case of [[Privacy Washing]].

**Why This Matters:** **[[Privacy as a Premium|Privacy as a premium]]** shows that "free" often means paying with your data, and that paying with money does not always stop the tracking. On Facebook and Instagram in the EU you can now also use the apps for free with less of your data used for adverts. Meta changed that option after [a €200 million fine](<Brussels Effect> "Read on in Brussels Effect: how European rules reach companies far beyond Europe, and where they stop.") from ==the European Commission==[^ec], ==which Meta is appealing== ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium#10. Meta Platforms, Inc., Annual Report (Form 10-K) for the year ended 31 December 2025|Meta, 2026]]: Meta's annual report on the appeal). [[Privacy by Design]], which ==the GDPR==[^gdpr] requires, makes the private option the default. A publication funded by its members can do without the advert trade altogether, as De Correspondent promised its readers in 2013, the model of [[Slow Media]]. The next time an app asks you to accept tracking or pay, ask whether a free option without tracking exists and, if you pay, whether the tracking really stops.

[^gdpr]: The General Data Protection Regulation: the EU's rules on how organisations may use personal data, and the rights people have over it. It has applied since 25 May 2018. [European Commission: 'Data protection in the EU'](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/data-protection-eu_en)
[^ec]: The EU's executive. Under the Digital Markets Act it can fine the largest platforms, the "gatekeepers", for not giving users a real choice, and it fined Meta €200 million for this. [[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium#6. The European Union hits Apple and Meta with 700 million euros in fines|AP: 'The European Union hits Apple and Meta with 700 million euros in fines']]
