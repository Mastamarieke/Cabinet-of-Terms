***The paid version has no ads. The free version watches everything you do.***

**Literal meaning:** A premium is the paid tier above the free one. **Privacy as a premium** puts not being tracked in that tier, and pays for the free version with data about what you do.

**Origin:** In 2010 Zizi Papacharissi called privacy *a luxury commodity*: free email and social networks are paid for with personal information, and staying private takes a computer literacy that follows income and education ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium|Papacharissi, 2010]]). The price became a number in November 2023. The EU Court of Justice had just ruled that Facebook users who refuse tracking must be offered an alternative, "if necessary for an appropriate fee", and Meta offered Facebook and Instagram without ads for €9.99 a month on the web and €12.99 in the app ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium|CJEU, 2023; Meta, 2024]]).

> Being watched is the default and not being watched is the upgrade: how much privacy you get depends on what you can afford.

**The Appeal:** Meta presented the choice as fairness: an internet paid for by adverts "gives people access to personalised products and services regardless of their economic status" ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium|Meta, 2024]]). That is true: the free tier is open to anyone with a phone.

**The Friction:** Who ends up in the free tier is not random. In a US survey by [[Madden-PrivacyAsAPremium-2017|Madden et al. (2017)]], 63% of smartphone users in households earning under $20,000 a year went online mostly by phone, against 21% of those earning $100,000 or more, and the poorer group used privacy settings less often (65% against 79%). Mobile access narrowed the [[Digital Divide]], the gap in who can get online and use it well, and left those it brought online easier to track. Paying does not always buy what it promises either: of 341 European websites with a pay-or-tracking wall, 32.9% still tracked paying users ([[MullerTribbensee-PrivacyAsAPremium-2024|Müller-Tribbensee, 2024]]). That is [[Privacy Washing]], a privacy promise that the practice does not keep. In April 2025 the European Commission fined Meta €200 million for offering only that choice ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium|AP, 2025]]).

**Why This Matters:** Once privacy has a price, "free" names who pays in data instead of money. It shows on the screen that asks you to choose before an app or a news site opens, and the question there is whether a third option exists, free and without tracking, and, if you pay, whether the tracking actually stops. On Facebook and Instagram in the EU there has been one since November 2024, free with less personalised adverts ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium|Meta, 2024]]), and after the fine Meta agreed with the Commission to rework that choice from January 2026 ([[Secondary-PrivacyAsAPremium|European Commission, 2025]]). [[Privacy by Design]], required by the GDPR, makes the private option the default; on the [[Fediverse]] no advertiser pays to reach you, though choosing a server takes the kind of know-how Papacharissi meant.
