## 1. Privacy as a luxury commodity

**Author:** Z. Papacharissi
**Year:** 2010
**Type:** Opinion essay
**Publisher:** First Monday, 15(8)
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.5210/fm.v15i8.3075

### What this source contributes

The concept before the business model. Free email and social networks are paid for with personal information, and privacy takes on the characteristics of a luxury commodity: inaccessible to most, costly to keep, and a social disadvantage to insist on. What makes it a luxury is the computer literacy it requires, which follows income, education and ethnicity, so that a *privacy divide* opens between haves and have-nots. Papacharissi's aim for regulation is to make privacy "a normal good — a good that everyone may afford, or even better, a public good". Published in First Monday's Op-Ed section, so not peer-reviewed.

## 2. Meta Platforms and Others v Bundeskartellamt, Case C-252/21

**Author:** Court of Justice of the European Union
**Year:** 2023
**Type:** Judgment (4 July 2023)
**Publisher:** EUR-Lex
**URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0252

### What this source contributes

Paragraph 150: users must be free to refuse consent to processing not necessary for the contract without having to stop using the social network, "which means that those users are to be offered, if necessary for an appropriate fee, an equivalent alternative not accompanied by such data processing operations." The phrase Meta later cited for its paid option.

## 3. Facebook and Instagram to Offer Subscription for No Ads in Europe

**Author:** Meta
**Year:** 2024
**Type:** Company statement (first published October 2023, updated 12 and 15 November 2024)
**Publisher:** Meta Newsroom
**URL:** https://about.fb.com/news/2023/10/facebook-and-instagram-to-offer-subscription-for-no-ads-in-europe/

### What this source contributes

The company's own account. The subscription without ads cost €9.99 a month on the web and €12.99 on iOS and Android, lowered in November 2024 to €5.99 and €7.99, when Meta also offered a free option with less personalised ads. Its reasoning: "We believe in an ad-supported internet, which gives people access to personalised products and services regardless of their economic status."

### Status

The phenomenon in its maker's words: quoted in The Appeal as the claim, not as evidence for it.

## 4. Opinion 08/2024 on Valid Consent in the Context of Consent or Pay Models Implemented by Large Online Platforms

**Author:** European Data Protection Board
**Year:** 2024
**Type:** Regulatory opinion (adopted 17 April 2024)
**Publisher:** EDPB
**URL:** https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-04/edpb_opinion_202408_consentorpay_en.pdf

### What this source contributes

Requested by the Dutch, Norwegian and Hamburg supervisory authorities. Conclusion: "In most cases, it will not be possible for large online platforms to comply with the requirements for valid consent if they confront users only with a binary choice between consenting to processing of personal data for behavioural advertising purposes and paying a fee." A further alternative without behavioural advertising, free of charge, should be given significant consideration.

## 5. Your Consent Is Worth 75 Euros A Year – Measurement and Lawfulness of Cookie Paywalls

**Author:** V. Morel, C. Santos, Y. Lintao & S. Human
**Year:** 2022
**Type:** Academic paper (peer-reviewed workshop)
**Publisher:** Proceedings of the 21st Workshop on Privacy in the Electronic Society (WPES '22), ACM
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3559613.3563205

### What this source contributes

Of 2,800 Central European websites, 13 used a cookie paywall, mostly news sites, at prices of 2.99 to 6.25 euros a month, or 36 to 75 euros a year (Der Standard: 75). The authors frame their lawfulness amid diverging decisions by data protection authorities. Note: a figure of 728 euros a year for 13 sites, found in press coverage, does not appear in the paper.

## 7. Meta commits to give EU users choice on personalised ads under Digital Markets Act

**Author:** European Commission
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Official statement (8 December 2025)
**Publisher:** Digital Markets Act website
**URL:** https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/meta-commits-give-eu-users-choice-personalised-ads-under-dma-2025-12-08_en

### What this source contributes

After the non-compliance decision of April 2025, Meta committed to give EU users "the effective choice between: consenting to share all their data and seeing fully personalised advertising, and opting to share less personal data for an experience with more limited personalised advertising", presented to users in January 2026. The Commission will collect evidence on its use and impact.

## 8. Privacy, Poverty, and Big Data: A Matrix of Vulnerabilities for Poor Americans

**Author:** M. Madden, M. Gilman, K. Levy & A. Marwick
**Year:** 2017
**Type:** Academic article (law review)
**Publisher:** Washington University Law Review
**URL:** https://openscholarship.wustl.edu/law_lawreview/vol95/iss1/6/

### What this source contributes

A nationally representative US telephone survey with an oversample of low-income adults. 63% of smartphone users in households earning under $20,000 a year mostly go online by phone, against 21% of those earning $100,000 or more; 65% of low-income social media users have used privacy settings to limit who sees their posts, against 79% in wealthier households. Poorer Americans are less likely to use the more expensive, more strongly encrypted phones. The survey predates pay-or-consent. Moved from primary to secondary on 2026-10-11, when European figures (Eurostat) took its place in the text; it remains the source of the opening of the semantic landscape.
