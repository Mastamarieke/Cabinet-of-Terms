***You can leave any time. So why haven't you?***

**Literal meaning:** A *vendor* is a seller, from French *vendre*, to sell. To be *locked in* is to be shut inside a room you cannot leave. Together the words describe a customer who walked in freely and can no longer walk out. **[[Vendor Lock-in|Vendor lock-in]]** is dependence on one supplier of technology. Switching has become so costly, in money, technology, contracts or plain effort, that leaving is no longer a real option, even when the supplier's interests drift away from yours.

**Origin:** The term comes from business computing. Organisations that built their work on one supplier's systems, such as SAP, Oracle or Salesforce, found that their data formats and connections made moving elsewhere too costly. Governments, hospitals and schools that handed core tasks to a technology company met the same problem. Ending the contract meant stopping the work.

> Dependence on one technology supplier so deep that leaving costs more than staying, whatever staying costs.

**The Appeal:** It starts as a good deal. The supplier knows the technology, works at scale and often does the job better than you could yourself. The lock grows slowly, with every file, account and habit that moves in. ==In a survey of 114 people working with cloud services in British businesses, fewer than half said they understood the term, and the cost of switching was often under-appreciated until it had to be paid== ([[OparaMartins-VendorLockIn-2016|Opara-Martins et al., 2016]]: Journal of Cloud Computing, a business survey on cloud lock-in).

**The Friction:** Because leaving costs more than staying, so you stay, even when it gets worse. ==In its 2024 case against Apple, the US Department of Justice argued that green message bubbles from Android phones keep teenagers on the iPhone, which 85 percent of them use== ([[Secondary-VendorLockIn#2. DOJ claims green bubbles are an issue in iPhone monopoly suit|TechCrunch, 2024]]: the antitrust suit of March 2024, quoting Apple's own emails). Cory Doctorow, the writer who coined [[Enshittification]], describes how platforms are good to their users at first and, once those users cannot leave, make the service worse to earn more from them. A school or ministry that is locked into American cloud services is also bound by an American law, the [[CLOUD Act]], which lets US authorities demand its data. Part of the lock is design. ==Cancelling Amazon Prime took so many pages that the process was known inside the company as "Iliad", after Homer's poem about a ten-year war== ([[Secondary-VendorLockIn#5. FTC Takes Action Against Amazon for Enrolling Consumers in Amazon Prime Without Consent and Sabotaging Their Attempts to Cancel|FTC, 2023]]: the US consumer regulator's case against Amazon). Researchers of [[Dark Patterns]], design that works against the user, call this a ==roach motel, easy to get into but difficult to get out of== ([[Gray-VendorLockIn-2024|Gray et al., 2024]]: an ontology of dark patterns, CHI), and it makes opting out possible on paper and nearly impossible in practice.

**Why This Matters:** Knowing the term **[[Vendor Lock-in|vendor lock-in]]** helps you see the cost of leaving before you are stuck, while leaving is still free. Since March 2024 ==iPhone users in the EU can install apps from other app stores, because the Digital Markets Act, a European law for the largest platforms, requires it== ([[Secondary-VendorLockIn#3. Apple releases iOS 17.4, bringing alternative marketplaces for the EU|Dotson, 2024]]: iOS 17.4), one of the European rules behind the [[Brussels Effect]]. On the [[Fediverse]], a network of independently run servers joined by an open protocol, you can move your account to another server and take your followers along, and ==the library of the University of Groningen left X in 2023 for the Mastodon server that SURF runs for Dutch education and research== ([[Secondary-VendorLockIn#4. The UB quits Twitter/X: follow us on Mastodon|University of Groningen, 2023]]: the library's announcement). ==In 2026 two Dutch journalism platforms, De Correspondent and Follow the Money, began a joint project to depend less on American tech companies, and the article announcing it had been written on an Apple laptop, edited in a Google word processor and published from Amazon's servers== ([[Secondary-VendorLockIn#6. De Correspondent en Follow the Money gaan samenwerken om onafhankelijker te worden van Amerikaanse big tech|De Correspondent, 2026]]: Wijnberg and Bijleveld on the project with Follow the Money). The next time you sign up, or your school or employer chooses a system, ask what it would cost to leave in five years and whether you can take your data with you.
