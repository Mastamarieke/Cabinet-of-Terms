## 1. Notes on Algorithmic Violence

**Author:** Mimi Onuoha
**Year:** 2018
**Type:** Essay (work in progress)
**Publisher:** GitHub, 7 February
**URL:** https://github.com/MimiOnuoha/On-Algorithmic-Violence

### What this source contributes

The essay that proposed the term: the violence that an algorithm or automated decision-making system inflicts by preventing people from meeting their basic needs, modelled on Johan Galtung's structural violence. Onuoha uses violence in its prohibitive sense, as what negatively shapes people's opportunities, not as physical force.

## 11. The Library of Missing Datasets

**Author:** Mimi Onuoha
**Year:** 2016
**Type:** Artwork (installation); described by ZKM and by Alex Estorick in Flash Art (2020)
**Publisher:** ZKM Center for Art and Media Karlsruhe (collection); Flash Art, 11 December 2020
**URL:** https://zkm.de/en/artwork/the-library-of-missing-datasets

### What this source contributes

A small filing cabinet with labelled folders that turn out to be empty: each names a dataset no one collects, from Onuoha's list of missing datasets on GitHub, such as people excluded from public housing because of criminal records. Missing datasets are, in her words, "the blank spots that exist in spaces that are otherwise data-saturated", and they typically fall where people are most vulnerable; "Spots that we've left blank reveal our hidden social biases and indifferences." Flash Art: https://flash---art.com/2020/12/algorithmic-violence-and-the-politics-of-data-the-work-of-mimi-onuoha-and-gretchen-andrew/ · list: https://github.com/MimiOnuoha/missing-datasets · the artist's website, where the work is listed as 2016–ongoing: https://www.mimionuoha.com

### Status

The phenomenon made visible as an object: an artwork, used in the semantic landscape.
