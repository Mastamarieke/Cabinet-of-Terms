## Safransky (2020) — Geographies of Algorithmic Violence: Redlining the Smart City

**Safransky, S. (2020).** Geographies of Algorithmic Violence: Redlining the Smart City. *International Journal of Urban and Regional Research*, 44(2). https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-2427.12833 (published online 2019)

---

### What this source contributes

City governments are turning to data-driven and algorithmic planning, which can depoliticise planning decisions. Safransky calls on urban studies to investigate geographies of algorithmic violence: a repetitive and standardised form of violence that contributes to the racialisation of space and the spatialisation of poverty. Her case is a proprietary market value assessment used to guide development in cities across the United States, an algorithm that helps officials decide which neighbourhoods to target for investment, disinvestment, and public service upgrades or disconnections. She argues that the racial, infrastructural and epistemological violence of this evaluation can lead to a new kind of municipal redlining.

### Analytical function in Algorithmic Violence

It answers the Friction question of what keeps the harm in place: the system repeats it, and it turns political choices into technical ones.
