***The algorithm flagged the family as fraudsters. Who is to blame?***

**Literal meaning:** An *algorithm* is a fixed series of steps that solves a problem; the word comes from the name of the ninth-century Persian mathematician al-Khwārizmī. *Violence* usually means physical force that causes harm. **[[Algorithmic Violence|Algorithmic violence]]** joins the two: harm done by following the steps, without anyone raising a hand. It names the harm of automated decision-making systems, such as a benefit payment refused, a dark face that face-tracking software did not see until ==Joy Buolamwini==[^gaze] put on a white mask, or a fraud check aimed at the wrong people.

[^gaze]: A researcher at MIT, who found it with the face-tracking software for her art project, the Aspire Mirror, and called it the *coded gaze*: systems that carry the biases of the people who build them. In 2018 her study with Timnit Gebru found error rates of up to 34.7 percent for darker-skinned women, against 0.8 percent for lighter-skinned men. [MIT News: 'Study finds gender and skin-type bias in commercial artificial-intelligence systems'](https://news.mit.edu/2018/study-finds-gender-skin-type-bias-artificial-intelligence-systems-0212) [Wikipedia: 'Joy Buolamwini'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Buolamwini)

**Origin:** The artist ==Mimi Onuoha==[^onuoha] proposed the term in 2018, in an essay about what happens when institutions take up big data and AI, which she described as =="the subsequent incorporation and leveraging of all these things into a hierarchical and unequal society"== ([[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#1. Notes on Algorithmic Violence|Onuoha, 2018]]: Notes on Algorithmic Violence, on GitHub). She built on *structural violence*, Johan Galtung's term from 1969 for harm done by the way a society is arranged rather than by one person. Researchers have since asked ==how such systems make violence look legitimate== ([[Bellanova-AlgorithmicViolence-2021|Bellanova et al., 2021]]: a collective discussion in international political sociology).

[^onuoha]: A Nigerian American artist and academic in New York whose work examines what data collection and technology do to society. [Wikipedia: 'Mimi Onuoha'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimi_Onuoha) [Mimi Onuoha: website](https://www.mimionuoha.com)

> Harm done by automated systems that carry existing inequalities, where the blame lands on the people nearest the system, not on those who designed it.

**The Appeal:** Automated systems promise the same rules for everyone, without a tired official, favourites or a bad day, so a government can check millions of applications quickly and in the same way. Behind that promise lies the belief that a computer cannot discriminate ([[Dataism]]). It also attracts those who want to move people at scale: in April 2025 the acting director of ==ICE==[^ice] said deportation should run [["Like Prime, but with human beings."|"like Prime, but with human beings"]] ([[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#10. ICE Head Wants Deportations to Run Like Amazon Prime: 'We Need to Treat This Like a Business'|Latin Times, 2025]]: Todd Lyons at the Border Security Expo in Phoenix), which treats people as parcels, a bureaucratic form of [[Dehumanization]].

[^ice]: United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement, the federal agency that enforces immigration law. Its acting director from 2025 was Todd Lyons. [Wikipedia: 'U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement)

**The Friction:** When the system is wrong, the blame lands on whoever is closest to it, the official who signs the letter, while the people who chose the data and wrote the rule stay out of sight: a ==moral crumple zone==[^elish]. In the Netherlands it happened twice, to parents in the ==childcare benefits scandal==[^toeslagen] and to students at ==DUO==[^duo], where a risk model picked people out more often because of their nationality or migration background. The government had handed a decision to a calculation ([[Cybernetic Governance]]), and the people it hit could not see how that calculation worked ([[Black Box]]). The harm keeps returning because ==the system repeats it== ([[Safransky-AlgorithmicViolence-2020|Safransky, 2020]]: the geographer calls algorithmic violence repetitive and standardised, and shows how an algorithm that tells US cities where to invest turns political choices into technical ones).

Governments also hand the calculation to companies. ==The Dutch police bought software from Palantir in secret, and more than 99 percent of the documents released about it was blacked out== ([[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#14. Minister: Nederlandse politie maakt gebruik van software Palantir|Security.NL, 2025]]: the justice minister's answers to parliamentary questions, August 2025), so the state depends on a supplier it cannot look into ([[Sovereignty as a Service]]). Palantir, co-founded by ==Peter Thiel==[^thiel], also builds ==ImmigrationOS==[^immos] for ICE, which Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor place in ==a politics that turns visions of disaster into votes==[^klein], the politics they call [[Endtime Fascism|end times fascism]].

[^elish]: Madeleine Clare Elish's term (2019): like the crumple zone of a car, which absorbs the impact so the rest stays intact, the person nearest an automated system takes the moral and legal blame when it fails, even with little control over what it did. [[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#17. Moral Crumple Zones: Cautionary Tales in Human-Robot Interaction|Elish: 'Moral Crumple Zones: Cautionary Tales in Human-Robot Interaction']]
[^immos]: Short for Immigration Lifecycle Operating System, software that Palantir builds for ICE under a $30 million contract of April 2025. According to the contract documents it is meant to streamline the "selection and apprehension" of people and to follow "self-deportations" in "near real-time". [[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#18. All the Ways Palantir is Assisting Trump's Abusive Removal Campaign|ACLU: 'All the Ways Palantir is Assisting Trump's Abusive Removal Campaign']]
[^thiel]: An American investor who co-founded PayPal and Palantir. [Wikipedia: 'Peter Thiel'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thiel)
[^klein]: In End Times Fascism (2026) Klein and Taylor describe politicians who promise "bunkered garrison states", with migration drawing most of the attention, while tech leaders preach that only the AI "arms race" can save humanity. [[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#12. End Times Fascism by Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor review – the fightback starts here|The Guardian: 'End Times Fascism by Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor review']]

[^toeslagen]: The Tax and Customs Administration's risk model made people without Dutch nationality more likely to be wrongly labelled as fraudsters. In 2021 Amnesty International called it a violation of human rights, and the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens fined the tax authority €2.75 million for its unlawful use of dual nationality. [[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#3. Amnesty ziet mensenrechtenschending bij toeslagenaffaire en vreest voor herhaling|NOS: 'Amnesty ziet mensenrechtenschending bij toeslagenaffaire en vreest voor herhaling']] [[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#5. Boete voor Belastingdienst van 2,7 miljoen voor discriminatie toeslagenouders|NOS: 'Boete voor Belastingdienst van 2,7 miljoen voor discriminatie toeslagenouders']]

[^duo]: From 2012 to 2023 DUO, which pays student finance, selected students with a migration background for fraud checks sooner than others. After reporting by NOS op 3 and Investico, an investigation found indirect discrimination, "unintended and unconscious", and in 2024 the government paid around ten thousand students back, €61 million in all. [[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#4. Geld terug voor studenten na discriminerende fraudeopsporing|NOS: 'Geld terug voor studenten na discriminerende fraudeopsporing']]

**Why This Matters:** Knowing the term **[[Algorithmic Violence|algorithmic violence]]** helps you see that a refused benefit payment or a fraud letter can be the work of a rule someone wrote, not bad luck. Makers can ask who a system will harm before anything is built, through [[Ethical Design]] and [[Design Justice]], which put dignity and the people most likely to be harmed at the start of the design. The law is following, slowly: the EU's AI Act [classes systems that decide on public benefits as high-risk](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/ "Read Annex III of the AI Act, point 5(a)."), ==even if those rules now wait until December 2027==[^omnibus]. The next time a letter says the system has selected you, ask who made the rule, not only who sent the letter, and how to reach a person who can change it; if no one can tell you, the decision is hiding behind the system.

[^omnibus]: In 2026 the EU postponed the AI Act's rules for high-risk systems to December 2027, in the Digital Omnibus on AI, after delays in standards and supervision. [[Secondary-AlgorithmicViolence#15. Digital Omnibus on AI|European Parliament: 'Digital Omnibus on AI']]

**Related terms:** [[Surveillance Capitalism]] (companies that turn behaviour into predictions and sell them: a fraud risk score is the same kind of prediction, used by a government to choose whom to check) · [[Data Brokers]] (companies that assemble and sell personal profiles: scores built from such data decide on loans, jobs and housing, opaque and without oversight) · [[Digital Exclusion]] (being shut out of digital services: a system can do it by not seeing you, as the face tracker that saw Joy Buolamwini only when she put on a white mask) · [[VSD (Value Sensitive Design)]] (design that writes human values into a system from the start: the place where DUO's three "neutral" criteria, age, type of education and distance to the parents, would have been questioned) · [[Biometric Surveillance]] (recognising people by face or body: the same face recognition that missed Buolamwini's face has, in police use, led to wrongful arrests) · [[Social Credit System]] (scores that reward or punish behaviour: a fraud risk score is one too, without the name) · [[Deadnaming]] (using a trans person's old name: a search engine can do it without anyone choosing to) · [[Fair Patterns]] (interface design in the user's interest: the screen or letter that delivers an automated decision could make objecting as easy as accepting) · [[Disaster Nationalism]] (crises used to justify nationalist consolidation: the politics of disaster that, in Klein and Taylor's words, ImmigrationOS serves) · [[e-acc (Effective Accelerationism)]] (the belief that technology should be sped up at any cost: the tech leaders who preach that only the AI race can save humanity) · [[Brussels Effect]] (European rules that travel abroad: the AI Act's rules on high-risk systems may reach beyond Europe through large US companies) · [[Vendor Lock-in]] (dependence on one supplier so deep that leaving costs more than staying: a police force that bought its analysis software in secret cannot easily replace it)
