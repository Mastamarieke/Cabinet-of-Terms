## Komanduri et al. (2011) — Of passwords and people: measuring the effect of password-composition policies

**Komanduri, S., Shay, R., Kelley, P. G., Mazurek, M. L., et al. (2011). Of passwords and people: measuring the effect of password-composition policies.** *CHI '11*. https://doi.org/10.1145/1978942.1979321

---

### What this source contributes

A study with more than 5,000 participants, who each created a password under one of four policies and were asked to recall it days later. The authors find "that a number of commonly held beliefs about password composition and strength are inaccurate": "a 16-character minimum with no additional requirements provides the most entropy while proving more usable on many measures than the strongest alternative", the policy demanding capitals, lower case, a symbol and a digit. Under that strict policy, 35% said their password was difficult to remember, against 23% under the sixteen-character minimum; stricter rules were also found more annoying.

### Analytical function in Security Theater

The evidence beneath the story of the password rules: the rule that looked strongest and was hardest to live with was not the one that protected most. It makes the entry's hope concrete, in Why This Matters, and gives the landscape its wonder.
