## Glasze et al. (2022) — Contested Spatialities of Digital Sovereignty

**Glasze, G., Cattaruzza, A., Douzet, F., et al. (2022). Contested Spatialities of Digital Sovereignty.** *Geopolitics*. https://doi.org/10.1080/14650045.2022.2050070

---

### What this source contributes

A forum of essays from political geography, law, computer science and ethics on *digital sovereignty* in Russia and the EU. Cloud computing and platforms, the authors argue, show that "the traditional coupling of concepts of sovereignty, territoriality and the state, of jurisdiction and borders, must be rethought." On the *Microsoft Ireland* case: data "are stored in infrastructures that are located on the territory of states", and in that case "the territorialisation of data collided with the repartition of jurisdiction", a collision "rapidly becoming an everyday issue". On France: cloud computing "was initially perceived only as a technical object", and a "poor assessment of the socioindustrial context and the technology itself" made the project of a "French cloud" fail; once the power at stake became clearer, France and Germany started Gaia-X.

### Analytical function in CLOUD Act

The mechanism behind the name. The entry's question, where a server stands against whose law reaches it, is the collision the authors describe; their account of the failed French cloud is the thread the landscape follows.
