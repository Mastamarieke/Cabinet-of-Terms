## Christakis & Terpan (2021) — EU–US negotiations on law enforcement access to data: divergences, challenges and EU law procedures and options

**Christakis, T. & Terpan, F. (2021). EU–US negotiations on law enforcement access to data: divergences, challenges and EU law procedures and options.** *International Data Privacy Law*. https://doi.org/10.1093/idpl/ipaa022

---

### What this source contributes

An analysis of the negotiations that the EU and the US began in September 2019 on an agreement for law enforcement access to data. The authors set out how the CLOUD Act turned the disclosure orders of the *Microsoft Ireland* case into law, applying "regardless of whether such communication, record, or other information is located within or outside of the United States", and how that "might conflict with existing European law (including the GDPR or national blocking statutes)". They also describe the EU's own answer: on 17 April 2018 the European Commission introduced *E-Evidence*, a legislative package to obtain electronic evidence directly from service providers.

### Analytical function in CLOUD Act

The appeal and its mirror. The source shows that the reach the CLOUD Act gives US investigators is something Europe wants as well (e-Evidence, a month later), and where that reach collides with EU law. It keeps the entry from making the United States the only actor. Read for this entry: the key points and the passages on the CLOUD Act and e-Evidence, not the full article.
