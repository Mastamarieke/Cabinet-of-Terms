***The ideology that data always outweighs human judgement. And that more data is always better.***

**Literal meaning:** **Dataism** is the ideological position that data-driven decisions are inherently superior to decisions based on human judgement, that more data is always better, and that expanding data collection is by definition social progress. The term was used by **Yuval Noah Harari** in *Homo Deus* (2016) to describe an emerging belief system.

**Origin:** **Harari** introduced the term in 2016 to describe a worldview in which data is treated as the universe's most valuable product, and information flows as the primary measure of worth. The term circulates more broadly in critical technology studies to name a set of unspoken assumptions that drive the data economy: that quantification produces objectivity, that more data guarantees better outcomes, and that data collection itself is neutral.

> The ideology that equates quantification with objectivity and data collection with progress, and so hides what gets measured, by whom, and for whose benefit.

**The Appeal:** **Dataism** rests on real insights. Data-driven decisions can reduce human cognitive bias. Large-scale data analysis reveals patterns that qualitative approaches miss. More information is, in many contexts, better than less.

**The Friction:** **Dataism** hides the choices behind the numbers: which variables are counted, whose lives are easiest to measure, which outcome is optimised. When exams in England were cancelled in 2020, an algorithm set students' grades partly from their school's past results; close to 40 percent of A-level grades came out lower than teachers had predicted, students at disadvantaged schools lost out more often, and within a week the government reversed the decision ([Reynolds, 2020](https://www.cnn.com/2020/08/17/europe/uk-school-exams-coronavirus-gbr-intl)). That is [[Algorithmic Violence]], harm done by an automated system that presents itself as neutral, and what Donna Haraway described in 1985 as [[Informatics of Domination]]: old hierarchies carried on in information systems. The belief also pays. [[Surveillance Capitalism]], Shoshana Zuboff's term for selling predictions made from behavioural data, rests on the idea that more data is always better, and [[Data Brokers]] trade that data between companies most people have never heard of. Staying outside it costs money: under [[Privacy as a Premium]] not being tracked becomes something you buy, as when Meta asked European users to pay at least 10 euros a month to avoid ads based on their data, which the European Commission fined it 200 million euros for ([AP, 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/23/nx-s1-5373560/apple-meta-eu-tech-fines)). Two things work against **dataism**: [[Situated Knowledge]], Haraway's point that all knowledge, data included, comes from a position, and [[AI Literacy]], knowing that a model's answer is produced rather than found.

**Why This Matters:** **Dataism** names the ideology that renders data collection and use invisible as political choices. Once you can identify the ideology, you can ask the questions it obscures: what data is collected, by whom, for whose benefit, and what goes unmeasured?
