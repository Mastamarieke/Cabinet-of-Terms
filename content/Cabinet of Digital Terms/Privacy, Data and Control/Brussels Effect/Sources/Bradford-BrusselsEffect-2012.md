## Bradford (2012) — The Brussels Effect

**Bradford, A. (2012).** The Brussels Effect. *Northwestern University Law Review*, 107. https://scholarship.law.columbia.edu/faculty_scholarship/271/

---

### What this source contributes

The article that named the Brussels Effect. Bradford examines the global power the EU exercises through its legal institutions and standards, and how it exports that influence without international institutions or other nations' cooperation: its regulations become entrenched in the legal frameworks of developed and developing markets alike, a "Europeanization" of important parts of global commerce. The article sets out the precise conditions and the mechanism through which this happens, and explains why the EU is the only jurisdiction that can wield such unilateral influence across areas from antitrust and privacy to health and environmental regulation, and why markets, other states and international institutions can do little to constrain it.

### Analytical function in Brussels Effect

It answers the Origin question: who named the mechanism, and how it works. The entry's Literal meaning (companies applying one standard everywhere because it is cheaper) is the mechanism this article describes; the book of 2020 extends it.
