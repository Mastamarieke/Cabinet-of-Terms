title: "Platform Governance & Regulation"
---

***The platform wrote the rules. Now governments are writing theirs. The question is which rules arrive first, and who enforces them.***

This cluster maps the legal and governance frameworks through which platform power is contested, constrained, or legitimised. The terms here describe the mechanisms of accountability: the legislation that determines what platforms must do, the international agreements that govern data flows, the regulatory influence that travels across borders, and the enforcement gaps that platforms exploit.

The vocabulary of platform governance matters because it defines who is responsible for what. Without it, harm is attributed to individual users, platform decisions are framed as neutral moderation, and structural accountability disappears.

The question this cluster asks: who makes the rules, and who is subject to them?

## Terms in this cluster

### [[Brussels Effect]]
The EU as global regulatory standard-setter. When European rules become the default for global platforms, regulation travels without a treaty.

### [[CLOUD Act]]
US legislation requiring American companies to hand over data stored abroad. The law that made cloud storage a geopolitical instrument.

### [[Deplatforming]]
*(also in: Culture Wars and Political Language)* — Removal of users or content by platform decision. Governance without judicial review.

### [[Algorithmic Auditor]]
*(also in: New Digital Professions)* — Independent assessment of algorithmic systems for bias and harm. The external review the system would not commission itself.
