# Tech Names from Fiction

Science fiction did not just inspire the ideas behind Silicon Valley — it supplied the vocabulary. Company names, product names, and entire technical terms were lifted directly from novels, films, and games. What the founders read as teenagers, they later branded as adults.

> "The names they chose reveal what they believe they are building." — on Thiel's Lord of the Rings network

---

## Company Names from Fiction

### The Tolkien Network (Peter Thiel)

Peter Thiel has reportedly read *The Lord of the Rings* over ten times. His investing network reflects this:

| Company | Founder / Backer | Fictive origin | Source |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|--------|
| **Palantir** | Peter Thiel (2003) | *Palantíri* — seeing stones used for long-distance surveillance in Middle-earth | Tolkien, *The Lord of the Rings* (1954) |
| **Anduril** | Palmer Luckey (2017) | *Andúril* — Flame of the West, the reforged sword of Aragorn | Tolkien, *The Lord of the Rings* (1954) |
| **Mithril Capital** | Peter Thiel | Mithril — the near-indestructible metal mined in Moria | Tolkien, *The Lord of the Rings* (1954) |
| **Narya Capital** | JD Vance | *Narya* — one of the three Elven rings of power | Tolkien, *The Lord of the Rings* (1954) |
| **Erebor Bank** | Palmer Luckey / Thiel-backed | Erebor — the Lonely Mountain, stronghold of the dragon Smaug | Tolkien, *The Hobbit* (1937) |

The pattern is not coincidental. These companies — spanning surveillance software, autonomous weapons, and crypto banking — cluster around Thiel's ideological network. The choice of Middle-earth names signals membership.

### The Culture Network (Elon Musk)

Musk cited Iain M. Banks' *Culture* series as one of his favourite SF works. Banks wrote the Culture as a post-scarcity socialist utopia run by benevolent AI. Musk read it as a blueprint.

| Name | Type | Fictive origin | Source |
|------|------|----------------|--------|
| **Just Read the Instructions** | SpaceX drone ship | Culture General Contact Unit from *The Player of Games* | Banks (1988) |
| **Of Course I Still Love You** | SpaceX drone ship | Culture General Contact Unit from *The Player of Games* | Banks (1988) |
| **A Shortfall of Gravitas** | SpaceX drone ship | Culture ship name, announced 2018 | Banks, Culture series |
| **Excession** | Musk family office | *Excession* (1996) — a Culture novel about an object beyond comprehension | Banks (1996) |

In the Culture series, ships choose their own names to express their character. Musk adopted this logic directly for rocket landing platforms. Banks died in 2013; Musk named the ships as a posthumous tribute.

## Terms Coined by Science Fiction, Now Standard Tech

Some words now used daily in tech were invented by SF authors. The industry adopted them without always acknowledging where they came from.

| Term | Coined by | Year | SF source | Now used for |
|------|-----------|------|-----------|--------------|
| **Robot** | Karel Čapek | 1920 | Play *R.U.R.* (Rossum's Universal Robots) | Autonomous machines |
| **Cyberspace** | William Gibson | 1982 | *Burning Chrome*; expanded in *Neuromancer* (1984) | The internet / networked virtual space |
| **Metaverse** | Neal Stephenson | 1992 | *Snow Crash* | Persistent virtual worlds (Meta, VR) |
| **Avatar** | Neal Stephenson | 1992 | *Snow Crash* — digital embodiment in the metaverse | User profile images, VR representations |
| **Android** | SF literature | 19th–20th c. | Popularized by Asimov and Philip K. Dick | Google's mobile OS; humanoid robots |
| **Ansible** | Ursula K. Le Guin | 1966 | *Rocannon's World* | IT automation tool (Red Hat) |

## Patroon

Het patroon is niet random. De bedrijven en producten die namen lenen van SF signaleren een specifieke *zelfopvatting*: de founders zien zichzelf als de helden uit hun favoriete verhalen. Thiel als Aragorn. Musk als de bouwers van Culture-schepen. De dystopieën die ze claimen te voorkomen, bouwen ze intussen na.
