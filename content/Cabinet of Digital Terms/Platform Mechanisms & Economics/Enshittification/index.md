***Your feed used to show your friends. Now it shows ads and posts you never asked for. Why don't you just leave?***

**Literal meaning:** **[[Enshittification]]** is built from three parts: *en-*, to make into, *shit*, and *-ification*, the process of becoming ([Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/enshittification "Read the Wiktionary entry for enshittification.")). Literally it is the process of turning something to shit. There are scattered uses from 2013, but the writer ==Cory Doctorow==[^doctorow] gave the word its meaning: not that a service is bad, but that it becomes bad in a fixed order, and for a reason. In Dutch it is called *platformverval*, platform decay, the polite name that researchers use as well.

**Origin:** Doctorow first used the word in this sense on his blog in November 2022 ([Pluralistic, 28 November 2022](https://pluralistic.net/2022/11/28/enshittification/ "Read Doctorow's first post on enshittification.")), and two months later he described the order in *Wired*, with TikTok as his case ([[Secondary-Enshittification#2. The 'Enshittification' of TikTok|Doctorow, 2023]]: the essay that made the word known). The American Dialect Society chose **[[Enshittification|enshittification]]** as its word of the year for 2023, and Australia's Macquarie Dictionary for 2024. In 2025 Doctorow worked the idea out in a book of the same name ([[Doctorow-Enshittification-2025|Doctorow, 2025]]: the stages, the causes and the cure, written by the maker of the word).

[^doctorow]: Canadian writer and activist, for many years at the Electronic Frontier Foundation, a digital rights organisation. Author of novels and of books on Big Tech, among them The Internet Con (2023) and Enshittification (2025). [Wikipedia: Cory Doctorow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cory_Doctorow)

> A platform serves its users until they cannot leave, then worsens the service for its advertisers and sellers, and finally squeezes them too.

**The Appeal:** The word says out loud what many people already suspected, and Doctorow opens his book with it: *It's not just you. The internet is getting worse, fast.* He enjoys that it is a rude word, but he argues that it spread because it explains something: the decline is not fate, it is the result of decisions taken by people who can be named. A vague annoyance gets a cause, and a cause can be changed.

**The Friction:** You don't leave because your friends would have to leave with you, at the same moment and to the same place, and each of them has their own friends to convince; users, Doctorow writes, take one another hostage. Facebook is his clearest example. In 2006 it drew people away from ==MySpace==[^myspace] with a promise: ==we will never spy on you== ([[Doctorow-Enshittification-2025|Doctorow, 2025]]: his case study of Facebook, which also shows the promise was real). That was the first stage: good to users. Once enough of them were locked in, the second stage began, good to business customers: Facebook sold advertisers ads aimed with what it knew about its users, and pushed publishers' posts into the feeds of people who had not asked for them. In the third stage it turned on those business customers too: ads cost more and reached the wrong people, and publishers had to pay to reach their own followers. In the fourth, ==the posts of the friends you chose to follow shrank to what Doctorow calls a homeopathic residue, and ads filled the space== ([[Doctorow-Enshittification-2025|Doctorow, 2025]]: the end of his Facebook case). The platform does not die, because no one can leave on their own: the dependence that the Cabinet calls [[Vendor Lock-in]]. What it costs you is more than an emptier feed. A platform ==you think with==[^google] ==leaves you worse off, once it decays, than before you started using it== ([[Ardoline-Enshittification-2025|Ardoline & Lenzo, 2025]]: two philosophers on platform decay as harm to memory and attention), a form of [[Deskilling]] that no user chose.

[^myspace]: The largest social network in the world from 2005 to 2009, bought by Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation in 2005. To win its users over, Facebook offered a bot: give it your MySpace password and it fetched your MySpace messages into Facebook, so you could leave without losing your friends there. [Wikipedia: Myspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myspace)

[^google]: Ardoline and Lenzo take Google as their example. Once you remember where to find something rather than the thing itself, the search engine has become part of your memory, and a search page full of ads damages both. [Ardoline & Lenzo (2025), Ethics and Information Technology](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10676-025-09846-1)

**Why This Matters:** You notice it when a service you rely on adds a step, an ad or a fee, and leaving would cost you your friends, your history or your files. Then ask two questions: can I take what is mine with me, and who decided this? For the people who build platforms, Ardoline and Lenzo put the question as [[Ethical Design]]: platforms that do not work against the people who depend on them. Elsewhere the first question already has an answer. In the [[Fediverse]], social networks spread over thousands of servers that talk to each other like e-mail, ==you can move your account to another server and your followers come with you, although your posts stay behind== ([[Secondary-Enshittification#11. Moving or leaving accounts|Mastodon, n.d.]]: the help page of Mastodon, the best-known network in the Fediverse).
