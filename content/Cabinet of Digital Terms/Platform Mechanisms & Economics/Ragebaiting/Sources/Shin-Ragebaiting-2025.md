## Shin et al. (2025) — Emotion Sells: Rage Bait vs. Information Bait in Clickbait News Headlines on Social Media

**Shin, J., DeFelice, C. & Kim, S. (2025).** Emotion Sells: Rage Bait vs. Information Bait in Clickbait News Headlines on Social Media. *Digital Journalism*. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392038337

---

### What this source contributes

Empirical study comparing the engagement performance of **rage-bait headlines** (designed to provoke outrage) vs. **information-bait headlines** (designed to signal informational value) in news content on social media. Key finding: **rage-bait headlines generate significantly higher engagement than information-bait headlines**, regardless of the underlying accuracy or informational quality of the content.

The study establishes that the ragebaiting mechanism operates specifically at the level of the headline — the first point of contact between content and audience — and that emotional arousal (anger, outrage) outperforms curiosity or informativeness as a click and share driver.

### Analytical function in Ragebaiting

Shin et al. (2025) extends Brady et al. (2017) from social media posts to journalism: the ragebaiting mechanism is not limited to influencer content or political commentary — it shapes news headline design in professional media contexts. This confirms that the incentive is structural to the attention economy as a whole, not confined to bad actors.

The contrast between rage-bait and information-bait is analytically useful in the entry: it names two distinct content strategies and shows which one the platform architecture rewards.
