***A fourteen-year-old does Cristiano Ronaldo's victory jump in front of the Palacio de Bellas Artes, and the crowd roars. Is he cool, or is he trying?***

**Literal meaning:** **[[Aurafarming]]** joins two words. *Aura* is slang for the presence someone gives off: calm, style, confidence. It is a cousin of [[Rizz]], without the flirting. *Farming* comes from video games, where ==you repeat the same action again and again to collect points== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#1. Aura farming|Dictionary.com, 2025]]: the slang dictionary on both halves of the word). Put together, the words make a joke at the expense of cool itself, because cool is supposed to look effortless and farming is nothing but effort. So ==when someone says you are aura farming, they are usually mocking you== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#12. Explain it to me quickly: What is aura farming, and is it cool or cringe?|Huynh, Ittimani & Gorman, 2025]]: Guardian explainer on cool and cringe).

**Origin:** One of the earliest uses is a TikTok bowling video captioned "Aura Farming", in January 2024. By September ==the phrase was all over X, where anime fans used it to mock characters who strike a pose to look cool== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#2. Aura Farming|Know Your Meme, 2025]]: the meme archive's entry, with the earliest posts). In July 2025 the word found a face: ==Rayyan Arkan Dikha==[^rayyan], eleven, dancing calmly at the bow of a racing boat at the Pacu Jalur festival in Riau, Indonesia. Millions watched his race, and in the [[Attention Economy]] what is watched is shown to more people. Creators and athletes around the world copied him, and ==fake accounts asked $30,000 for a "collaboration" in his name== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#4. The 'aura farming' boy danced for tradition, then the internet turned it into a trend and a trap|Souisa, 2025]]: ABC News on the boy, the trend and the scam). In the summer of 2026 the word went back from the screen to the street. ==Aura battles began in Brazil and spread to Lima, Mexico City, Quito, Buenos Aires and Madrid== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#7. 'Aura farming': The video game-inspired youth trend sweeping Latin America|Briceño & Suárez, 2026]]: Associated Press on the battles in Latin America and Spain): young people take turns in a circle, dancing and posing, and [the crowd is the jury](https://www.euronews.com/video/2026/08/21/mexico-aura-battles-turn-a-viral-meme-into-live-contests "Watch two boys face off in an aura battle in Mexico (Euronews, 2026)").

> Doing things on purpose to look cool and confident, as if collecting points in a game; often said to mock someone whose trying shows.

**The Appeal:** **[[Aurafarming]]** gives a light new name to something young people have always done, ==showing off in front of others, as a Mexican lecturer in social media points out== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#5. ¿Qué significa "farmear aura" y por qué es popular entre jóvenes?|Gutiérrez Cortés in Estrada, 2026]]: interview on the news site of the Tec de Monterrey). The battles add a game with clear rules: no touching, no insults, and "whoever gets the crowd to make more noise wins", as a Peruvian organiser told AP. In the circle everyone gets a turn in the middle, a moment of [[Main Character Energy]], and the crowd decides who keeps it. ==One winner in Mexico City said he did it less for the prize than "to have fun and get out of the house"== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#6. What is 'aura farming'? The story behind the viral street battles born on TikTok|Thykjaer, 2026]]: Euronews on the battle in Mexico City).

**The Friction:** Once there is a word for it, effortless cool becomes something you can be caught doing: the unposed pose of a [[Curated Life]], with the posing made visible. And someone keeps the score. In 2024 people posted how many ==aura points they had lost by tripping in front of others== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#3. You Might Be Losing 'Aura Points' and Not Even Know It|Lindsay, 2024]]: GQ on aura as a score you gain and lose), and [[Tierlists S-Tier|tierlists]] rank people from S to F in the same game vocabulary: [[Gamification Creep]], the points and leaderboards of apps, has reached the way you walk, laugh and fall. The score is not the same for everyone. In a Dutch school a video of a boy and a girl kissing went round the Snapchat groups, and ==the next day the girl was pelted with food, while for the boy it was "aura"== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#8. Niet appen maar snappen|Sahadat & Uijtewaal, 2026]]: de Volkskrant, girls of thirteen and fourteen on Snapchat). And Rayyan never chose to become a meme: ==his dance was a role in the race, and online it became a pose without its context== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#9. Aura farming with memetic communication: framing celebritism through memetic layers and the DIONS Nexus of Cyberstudies|Tilton, 2026]]: journal article on how the boy became a meme without his consent).

**Why This Matters:** **[[Aurafarming]]** shows that looking natural can be work, and that others hand out the points. Adults call the same work [[Personal Branding]]; young people gave it a name that laughs at it. But it can also lift someone up. For a twenty-year-old film student, ==aura farming is "letting your essence flow in a place where people are watching you, but where there is no shame"== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#7. 'Aura farming': The video game-inspired youth trend sweeping Latin America|Briceño & Suárez, 2026]]: Associated Press, a participant in the battles), and ==a game that began on a feed now gets teenagers out of the house== ([[Secondary-Aurafarming#6. What is 'aura farming'? The story behind the viral street battles born on TikTok|Tovar in Thykjaer, 2026]]: Euronews, a Venezuelan psychologist on the battles) to meet [[IRL (In Real Life)|in real life]]. Before you film or share someone else's moment of aura, ask whether they would want to be seen that way. The boy on the boat danced for the race and was picked up by the world without warning, and the girl in the schoolyard got food thrown at her for a video others passed round.

**Related terms:** [[Comparison Culture]] (measuring yourself against others: your aura points against theirs) · [[Imposter Syndrome]] (the other side: others look effortless, and you know your own effort) · [[Crash-out]] (the opposite moment: losing your cool in public, and that is filmed too)

[^rayyan]: An eleven-year-old from Riau, on Sumatra. In the Pacu Jalur race he is the *togak luan*: *togak* means upright and *luan* the bow. He stands at the front of the boat, urges the rowers on and dances when his boat leads. After the video went round, his local government gave him a scholarship (ABC News, 2025).
