## Zendle & Cairns (2018) — Video game loot boxes are linked to problem gambling: Results of a large-scale survey

**Zendle, D. & Cairns, P. (2018). Video game loot boxes are linked to problem gambling: Results of a large-scale survey.** *PLOS ONE*, 13(11), e0206767. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0206767

---

### What this source contributes

A survey of 7,422 gamers. The more they spent on loot boxes, items bought with real money whose contents are random, the more severe their problem gambling. The link was stronger than the link between problem gambling and buying other in-game items, which suggests that the gambling-like feature of loot boxes is what matters. The authors say plainly that the study cannot tell which way it runs: whether buying loot boxes leads to problem gambling, or whether problem gamblers are drawn to spending on loot boxes. In either case, they write, there may be good reason to regulate loot boxes.

### Analytical function in Predatory Design

The evidence for one weak spot: the thrill of chance. In The Friction the source carries the sentence on loot boxes, with the limit stated in the citation (a link, not proof of cause).
