## 1. Predatory Monetisation? A Categorisation of Unfair, Misleading and Aggressive Monetisation Techniques in Digital Games from the Player Perspective

**Author:** Elena Petrovskaya, David Zendle
**Year:** 2021
**Type:** Academic
**Publisher:** Journal of Business Ethics, 181(4), 1065–1081
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10551-021-04970-6

### What this source contributes

Games have become platforms for repeated spending instead of a product you buy once. The authors asked 1,104 players to describe a purchase that felt misleading, aggressive or unfair, and found 35 techniques in eight domains, among them game dynamics designed to drive spending, predatory advertising, in-game currency and pay to win. Several of these do not seem to be covered by UK consumer protection law.

## 2. How Predatory Monetization Designs Manifest in Child-Friendly Video Games

**Author:** Qiurong Song, Zinan Zhang, Rie Helene (Lindy) Hernandez, Xinning Gui, Yubo Kou (The Pennsylvania State University)
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Academic
**Publisher:** USENIX Symposium on Usable Privacy and Security (SOUPS 2025)
**URL:** https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2025/presentation/song

### What this source contributes

Two studies of Roblox, a game platform with an enormous number of child players: interviews with Roblox developers on how they see predatory monetization designs, and a walkthrough of how these designs show up in Roblox games. Many developers know these designs and use them.

## 3. Let's unbox! Gambling-like elements on Twitch and simulated gambling among Flemish teenagers

**Author:** Eva Grosemans, Tim Smits, Bieke Zaman, Rozane De Cock
**Year:** 2023
**Type:** Academic, extended abstract
**Publisher:** Proceedings of DiGRA 2023
**URL:** https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2124/2123/2120

### What this source contributes

A survey of 2,280 Flemish teenagers, between November 2021 and February 2022. 43.1% had watched loot box openings in the past year, boys much more often than girls. Those who watched were more inclined to open free loot boxes and to pay for them than those who did not.

## 4. An Ontology of Dark Patterns Knowledge: Foundations, Definitions, and a Pathway for Shared Knowledge-Building

**Author:** Colin M. Gray, Cristiana Teixeira Santos, Nataliia Bielova, Thomas Mildner
**Year:** 2024
**Type:** Academic
**Publisher:** ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642436

### What this source contributes

Ten existing taxonomies of dark patterns, from research and from regulators, brought together in one ontology with standard definitions for 64 types on three levels. Grinding appears in it as a dark pattern when a game offers to skip it for money.

## 5. Onderzoek internationale consumententoezichthouders toont aan: veel misleiding in online games

**Author:** Autoriteit Consument & Markt (ACM)
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Regulator, press release
**Publisher:** ACM, 30 June
**URL:** https://www.acm.nl/nl/publicaties/onderzoek-internationale-consumententoezichthouders-toont-aan-veel-misleiding-online-games

### What this source contributes

Consumer authorities from several countries checked 439 online and mobile games for misleading and aggressive practices. Of the games with an in-game currency, only 7% showed players what they had spent in total, and only 2% let players turn leftover currency back into money. 38% used messages about scarcity to push purchases, and some of those messages were fake. 62% of the games with loot boxes were advertised as suitable for children.

## 6. Genshin Impact Game Developer Will be Banned from Selling Lootboxes to Teens Under 16 without Parental Consent, Pay a $20 Million Fine to Settle FTC Charges

**Author:** Federal Trade Commission
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Regulator, press release
**Publisher:** FTC, 17 January
**URL:** https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/genshin-impact-game-developer-will-be-banned-selling-lootboxes-teens-under-16-without-parental

### What this source contributes

The maker of Genshin Impact agreed to pay $20 million and to stop selling loot boxes to teenagers under 16 without their parents' consent. The FTC said the game deceived players about the odds of winning prizes and about what opening loot boxes would cost, through several currencies with confusing exchange rates. The order still needed a judge's approval.

## 8. Age Appropriate Design: A Code of Practice for Online Services

**Author:** Information Commissioner's Office (ICO)
**Year:** 2021
**Type:** Regulator, code of practice
**Publisher:** ICO
**URL:** https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/

### What this source contributes

The UK code that asks apps, games, connected toys and websites likely to be used by children to put the best interests of the child first. One of its fifteen standards is about nudge techniques: they should not be used to get children to give up personal data or to weaken their privacy settings.

## 9. Loot box engagement: relationships with educational attainment, employment status and earnings in a cohort of 16 000 United Kingdom gamers

**Author:** James Close, Stuart Gordon Spicer, Laura Louise Nicklin, Joanne Lloyd, Helen Mary Lloyd
**Year:** 2022
**Type:** Academic
**Publisher:** Addiction
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/add.15837

### What this source contributes

An online survey of 16,196 adults in the UK who call themselves gamers. 17% bought loot boxes. Buying them, and the amount spent relative to earnings, went with younger age, being male, not having a university degree and being unemployed, and those who earned less spent a larger share of their monthly earnings on loot boxes. The authors note that these associations mirror those of other addictive and problematic behaviours. A cross-sectional survey of adults: it shows who spends more, not why.
