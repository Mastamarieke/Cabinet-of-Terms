## King et al. (2019) — Unfair play? Video games as exploitative monetized services: An examination of game patents from a consumer protection perspective

**King, D. L., Delfabbro, P., Gainsbury, S., Dreier, M., Greer, N. L. & Billieux, J. (2019). Unfair play? Video games as exploitative monetized services: An examination of game patents from a consumer protection perspective.** *Computers in Human Behavior*, 101, 131–143. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.017

---

### What this source contributes

A review of 13 patents for in-game purchasing systems, found on Google Patents, read against the consumer rights in the terms of use of the companies that hold them. Some of these systems can be called unfair or exploitative: they use what the company knows about the player (behavioural tracking) and data manipulation (such as adjusting prices) to tailor offers that keep players spending, while offering few or no guarantees, such as a refund. The authors warn that this could exploit vulnerable players, such as adolescents and problem gamers, and discuss the findings in relation to behavioural economics, addiction psychology and the clinical picture of gaming disorder. They call for consumer protection, psychologically informed interventions and ethical game design guidelines.

### Analytical function in Predatory Design

The mechanism. The patents show that the design is meant to learn where a player gives in and to adjust the offer there, which is the first sentence of The Friction, and they date the shift from games you buy once to games you keep paying for (Origin). The link to gaming disorder is the reason for that term in Related terms.
