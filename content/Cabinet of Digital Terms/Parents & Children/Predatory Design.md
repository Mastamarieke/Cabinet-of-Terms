***You meant to play for ten minutes and spend nothing. Who knew in advance where you would give in?***

**Literal meaning:** *Predatory* comes from the Latin *praedator*, a plunderer or hunter; in English it also means exploiting others for personal gain ([Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/predatory "Read the Wiktionary entry for predatory.")). The word is older in finance: *predatory lending* means [imposing unfair and abusive loan terms on borrowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predatory_lending "Read the Wikipedia article on predatory lending."). Put next to *design*, the hunt moves into the product itself: **[[Predatory Design|predatory design]]**. In games you pay with coins such as [[Robux]], and the bill stays out of sight: ==of the games with an in-game currency, only 7 percent showed players what they had spent in total== ([ACM, 2025](https://www.acm.nl/nl/publicaties/onderzoek-internationale-consumententoezichthouders-toont-aan-veel-misleiding-online-games): consumer authorities from several countries checked 439 online games).

**Origin:** Games stopped being something you buy once and became services you keep paying for. ==Researchers studied 13 patents for in-game purchase systems that track players and adjust offers and prices to keep them spending, and warned that they could exploit vulnerable players such as adolescents and problem gamers== ([King et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.017): a review of game patents from a consumer protection perspective). Two years later the practice had a name in a title, ==Predatory Monetisation?== ([Petrovskaya & Zendle, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10551-021-04970-6): 1,104 players described purchases that felt misleading, aggressive or unfair).

> Design that makes money from your weak spots: the thrill of chance, the fear of missing out, the wish to belong.

**The Appeal:** The game is free, the first rewards come fast, and everything looks like sweets, like the gingerbread house in *Hansel and Gretel*. On [[Roblox]] you build and play with friends, and the outfits for your avatar cost Robux. Streaks and daily rewards, the [[Gamification Creep]] of every app, bring you back tomorrow.

**The Friction:** It learns where you give in, and charges you there. A countdown on a skin, a friend who already owns it, a reward that only comes after hours of ==grinding==[^grind] unless you pay: on their own these are [[Dark Patterns]], now often called [[Deceptive Design]]. In games they are stacked, and that stacking is what makes it predatory: ==in Roblox, interface tricks and psychological tactics reinforce each other to push players to spend== ([Song et al., 2025](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2025/presentation/song): interviews with Roblox developers and a walkthrough of their games). [[Loot Boxes]] sell the [[Dopamine Feedback Loops|reward that sometimes comes and sometimes doesn't]] as a product, and ==the more players spent on them, the more severe their gambling problems== ([Zendle & Cairns, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0206767): survey of 7,422 gamers, a link, not proof of cause). ==In January 2025 the maker of Genshin Impact agreed to pay $20 million. Players had to change real money into one currency and then another, at odd exchange rates, before they could open a loot box, while promotions and influencer campaigns made the odds look better than they were. Some children spent hundreds or even thousands of dollars.== ([FTC, 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/genshin-impact-game-developer-will-be-banned-selling-lootboxes-teens-under-16-without-parental): the US consumer regulator, the settlement still needed a judge's approval) The stack reaches beyond the game, through ==streamers==[^stream] and the same [[Parasocial Relationship]] that makes a recommendation sound like a friend's. Countdowns work on [[FOMO]], and ==38 percent of the games checked used messages about scarcity, some of them fake== ([ACM, 2025](https://www.acm.nl/nl/publicaties/onderzoek-internationale-consumententoezichthouders-toont-aan-veel-misleiding-online-games): the same 439 games).

[^stream]: In Flanders, where paid loot boxes are banned, four in ten teenagers had watched streamers open them in the past year, in a survey of 2,280 Flemish teenagers. [Grosemans et al., 2023](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2124/2123/2120)

[^grind]: Repeating the same dull task in a game to unlock something, often for hours. Gray et al. list it as a dark pattern when the game offers to skip it for money. [Gray et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642436)

**Why This Matters:** The moment comes later, on a bank statement or in a parent's inbox, when the coins turn back into money. The question to ask is not *how do I resist* but *who built this to make me give in?* The same tools can work the other way: a game can show what you have spent, as [[Fair Patterns]] do with the reject button. And rules are catching up: ==since October 2026 PEGI rates existing games with paid loot boxes 16, also games that were rated 3, 7 or 12== ([PEGI, 2026](https://pegi.info/news/pegi-updates-age-ratings-existing-games-paid-random-items): the European age rating).
