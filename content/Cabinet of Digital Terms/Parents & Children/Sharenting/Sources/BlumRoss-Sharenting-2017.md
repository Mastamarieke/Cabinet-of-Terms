## Blum-Ross & Livingstone (2017) — "Sharenting," parent blogging, and the boundaries of the digital self

**Blum-Ross, A. & Livingstone, S. (2017).** "Sharenting," parent blogging, and the boundaries of the digital self. *Popular Communication*, 15(2), 110–125. https://doi.org/10.1080/15405702.2016.1223300

---

### What this source contributes

The authors ask whether sharenting is a form of digital self-representation. In interviews with seventeen parent bloggers they explore how parents draw the borders of their digital selves and justify what is their "story to tell". The bloggers face real ethical dilemmas: representing themselves as parents inevitably makes parts of their children's lives public, creating risks they are, paradoxically, responsible for guarding against. They weigh obligations to themselves, to their children now and in the future, and to their communities. Platforms, built around an individual idea of identity, make this relational self-representation harder rather than easier.

### Analytical function in Sharenting

This source keeps the Friction fair to parents: the problem is not carelessness but a structure in which the parent's story and the child's privacy cannot be separated, on platforms designed for individuals.
