## 1. Meet the 'cleanfluencers', the online gurus who like things nice and tidy

**Author:** Arwa Mahdawi
**Year:** 2019
**Type:** Journalism
**Publisher:** The Guardian, 29 January
**URL:** https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2019/jan/29/meet-the-cleanfluencers-the-online-gurus-who-like-things-nice-and-tidy

### What this source contributes

The article that put the word in print, in quotation marks, a month after *Tidying Up with Marie Kondo* started on Netflix. Mahdawi describes Mrs Hinch, then a 28-year-old hairdresser from Essex with 1.7 million followers, and her branded vocabulary: cleaning is *hinching*, buying products a *hinch haul*, her followers the #HinchArmy. When she praised a Minky M Cloth, it sold out for weeks and the company's website crashed; Minky confirmed a big rise in sales. The article also notes that the best-known cleanfluencers and their audiences are women, while British women still do 60% more housework than men, and quotes the sociologist Stephanie Baker on order and self-management in uncertain times.

**Year:** 2023
**Type:** Academic, conversation
**Publisher:** The Sociological Review Magazine, 12 December
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.51428/tsr.orya2355

The two authors of the primary source talk about their research. Casey: *"it is messy selves that are being fixed by cleaning."*

## 3. Unhinged consumerism or aspirational organisation? A critical discourse analysis of restock videos

**Author:** Giorgia Riboni, Helen Ringrow
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Academic, peer-reviewed
**Publisher:** Discourse & Society, 37(1), 153–169
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/09579265251336791

### What this source contributes

A critical discourse analysis of Instagram posts tagged #restocking: fridges, pantries and bathroom cabinets refilled in clear containers. The authors describe a "curated authenticity" and a passive form of consumption, and argue that the genre can hide overconsumption, waste and traditional gender roles. So far only the abstract and the authors' own blog (item 4) have been read; the article is paywalled and the author version has been requested. Once read, it is meant to become the second primary source of the entry.

## 4. What watching people restock their cupboards tells us about aesthetics and authenticity on social media

**Author:** Giorgia Riboni, Helen Ringrow
**Year:** 2026
**Type:** Academic, blog
**Publisher:** University of Portsmouth, 16 July (also in The Conversation)
**URL:** https://www.port.ac.uk/news-events-and-blogs/blogs/academic-expertise/what-watching-people-restock-their-cupboards-tells-us-about-aesthetics-and-authenticity-on-social-media

### What this source contributes

The authors of item 3 explain their study in plain language. Restock videos are heavily edited, with the sound turned up for an ASMR effect, often filmed from the viewer's point of view; products are chosen for their colour. *"The posts we analysed mainly showed wives and mothers organising their family homes"* (#OrganizedMum, #MomLife), although *"Restocking itself is not a gendered activity"*. And: *"Restock videos can be used to promote the purchase of both the products being organised and the bins organising them."*

## 5. As social media creators push overconsumption, some question whether 'eco' influencing is possible

**Author:** Katie Mather
**Year:** 2024
**Type:** Journalism
**Publisher:** Yahoo News, 22 April
**URL:** https://www.yahoo.com/news/as-social-media-creators-push-overconsumption-some-question-whether-eco-influencing-is-possible-225109768.html

### What this source contributes

On overconsumption in influencer content. Organisational accounts, *"referred to as 'cleanfluencers'"*, encourage overconsumption *"by showing creators moving products from their original plastic packaging into new, more aesthetically pleasing plastic containers"*. Deinfluencing, which went viral in early 2023 as an answer to sustainability concerns, *"isn't a plausible solution given how antithetical it is to the job"*.

## 6. TikTok Cleaning 'Product Overload' Trend Could Be Dangerous: Experts

**Author:** Matilda Head
**Year:** 2022
**Type:** Journalism
**Publisher:** Business Insider, 16 July
**URL:** https://www.businessinsider.com/tiktok-cleaning-product-overload-trend-could-be-dangerous-experts-2022-7

### What this source contributes

On product overload: TikTok videos, often tagged #cleantok, in which creators pour bleach, washing-up liquid, fabric softener and sprays together for the colours and the ASMR sound. Richard Leigh of Unblocktober, a UK campaign for drains and waterways: *"The amount of product used in a single one of these videos is more than many households would be using over six months."* A marine specialist explains how bleach in waterways forms dioxins; a pharmacist warns that mixed cleaning products *"can be lethal when ingested or inhaled"*. The article also gives #cleantok more than 40 billion views and Mrs Hinch more than 4 million followers on Instagram.

## 7. The impact of influencers on sustainable consumption: A systematic literature review

**Author:** Ana Cristina Munaro, Renato Hübner Barcelos, Eliane Cristine Francisco Maffezzolli
**Year:** 2024
**Type:** Academic, peer-reviewed (systematic review)
**Publisher:** Sustainable Production and Consumption, 52, 401–415
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spc.2024.10.024

### What this source contributes

A review of 52 studies on how influencers affect sustainable consumption. Influencers can make the purchase intentions, attitudes and behaviour of their followers more sustainable, especially when they are credible and expert and hold green attitudes themselves. Read: abstract and introduction.

## 11. Operation Niki (YouTube)

**Author:** Operation Niki
**Year:** 2022 to now
**Type:** Consumer product, YouTube channel
**Publisher:** YouTube
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/@operationniki

### What this source contributes

An American cleaning channel with 1.4 million subscribers (October 2026), described as *"JESUS Mom Wife / Cook Clean Organize Self Care / AMAZON LINKS EMAIL"*. A series tagged #hotelcleaning: *I have great respect for housekeeping but you know ya girl gotta do her thing!* (July 2023), *Another hotel visit… another hotel clean!* (July 2023), *Vacation essentials that make sense to ME!* (July 2024), *Turning my hotel room into my home away from home!* (October 2024), each with Amazon links in the description. *Making the best out of a not so good situation… Lemonade out of lemons* (August 2025), a clean-with-me in a hospital room, has 4.4 million views.

### Status

An artefact: the genre itself, with its sales links. Only what the channel shows publicly is used; nothing about the creator's private life.
