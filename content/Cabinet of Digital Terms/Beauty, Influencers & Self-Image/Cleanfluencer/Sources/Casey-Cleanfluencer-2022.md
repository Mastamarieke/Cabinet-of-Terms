## Casey & Littler (2022) — Mrs Hinch, the rise of the cleanfluencer and the neoliberal refashioning of housework: Scouring away the crisis?

**Casey, E. & Littler, J. (2022). ### What this source contributes

A study of Sophie Hinchliffe, known as Mrs Hinch, through her Instagram posts and her books. The authors take the word from The Guardian: *"The term 'cleanfluencer' therefore indicates an influencer who shares content about cleaning (Mahdawi, 2019)."* Mrs Hinch, a former hairdresser from Essex, has been on Instagram since March 2018, had 4.1 million followers by the time of writing, deals with Procter & Gamble and her own range at Tesco and Asda. She rarely uses the word *housework*; she calls it *hinching*.

Three arguments. First, an old script in new packaging: new ways of keeping women willing to do unpaid housework (*"Mr Hinch very rarely appears with a mop in his hand"*). Second, what they call spectacular housewifization: she became the breadwinner by performing the role of the non-earning housewife. Third, cleaning as therapy: *"There's something so therapeutic about folding!"*, routine and repetition as calm in an uncertain world, with the book *Hinch Yourself Happy* promising to soothe the soul. What stays out of view is the dirty work: with a snap of the fingers mess becomes clean, a form they call magical housework femininity. They close with a thought experiment: feeds in which men clean, and in which no environmentally damaging products of multinationals are promoted.

### Analytical function in Cleanfluencer

The word and the mechanism. The article gives the source of the term (Origin), the promise of calm and therapy (The Appeal), and the core of The Friction: the cleanfluencer is paid for performing the housework that stays unpaid for the women who watch.
