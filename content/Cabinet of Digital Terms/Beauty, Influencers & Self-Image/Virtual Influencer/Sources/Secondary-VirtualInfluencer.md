## 2. Sexy AI-influencers zijn niet zo onschuldig als ze lijken. 'Dit is een vorm van manipulatie en misleiding'

**Author:** R. De Coninck
**Year:** 2026
**Type:** Investigative journalism
**Publisher:** Trouw
**URL:** https://www.trouw.nl/redactie/AI_influencers/

### What this source contributes

Documents AI-generated personas posing as military personnel, nurses and police officers, using a uniform and a sympathetic backstory to draw followers, with sexually suggestive content linking through to paid platforms. Records the downstream harms: financial exploitation, disinformation, transphobic and racist content, and erosion of trust. Researchers quoted describe this explicitly as manipulation and misleading behaviour.

The strongest secondary source for the Friction section, because it shows the mechanism at its dark end rather than in its commercial form.

### Related entries

- [[Virtual Influencer]] — the fabricated persona deployed for exploitation rather than endorsement

## 5. Jongeren en... virtuele influencers

**Author:** Youngworks
**Year:** 2025
**Type:** Youth research
**Publisher:** Youngworks
**URL:** https://youngworks.nl/blog/jongeren-en-virtuele-influencers/

### What this source contributes

Dutch youth research agency reporting on young people's awareness of and attitudes toward virtual influencers. Documents that awareness is rising, but that emotional engagement with virtual personas remains comparable to engagement with human influencers for many young users.

That gap between knowing and feeling is the finding that matters: awareness does not neutralise the effect.

### Related entries

- [[Virtual Influencer]] — awareness rises without the emotional response diminishing
- [[Parasocial Relationship]] — the bond persists even where the persona is known to be fabricated
