***She posts from Bali on Monday, partners with Prada on Wednesday, and replies to your comment on Friday. She does not exist.***

**Literal meaning:** A **virtual influencer** is a computer-generated persona — built with CGI, AI, or motion capture — designed to function like a human content creator. It posts, collaborates with brands, and interacts with followers. No person is behind it.

**Origin:** The earliest virtual influencers appeared in the early 2010s. **Lil Miquela**, launched in 2016, has worked with **Calvin Klein**, **Prada**, and **Samsung**. **Lu do Magalu**, created for Brazilian retailer **Magazine Luiza** in 2009, remains one of the most-followed virtual personas globally. What began as a marketing experiment is now an industry — hundreds of active virtual influencers are currently documented across major platforms.

> A fabricated persona engineered to generate the trust and parasocial intimacy of a human influencer — without the limits of being one.

**The Appeal:** For brands, a virtual influencer offers total control. It never ages, never gets sick, never goes off-script, and never generates unplanned controversy. The persona is owned outright — a piece of intellectual property that can post indefinitely at any scale. For followers, the appeal is less visible: the warmth, the relatability, the sense of connection are real experiences, even if the source of them is not.

**The Friction:** [[Kim-2023|Kim et al. (2023)]] locate the mechanism in the CASA paradigm — *Computers Are Social Actors*: the more human a virtual agent appears, the more it activates automatic social trust responses — the same responses that evolved for real human relationships. [[Sands-2022|Sands et al. (2022)]] show that anthropomorphism here is not a side effect of good design — it is the design intention. The virtual influencer does not just look trustworthy. It is engineered to trigger the neural and social processes that produce trust automatically, even when the audience knows the persona is fabricated.

[[Parasocial Relationship]] — the one-sided emotional bond that develops between a follower and a media figure — is the mechanism being activated. Kim et al. (2023) found that this trust advantage disappears once AI origin is explicitly disclosed. That is precisely why disclosure is typically buried.

[[Recommender Systems]] accelerate the dynamic: by surfacing the same virtual persona repeatedly, the algorithm builds familiarity that makes the parasocial bond feel earned — without any actual interaction taking place.

[[Highlight Reel]] is every human influencer's tool: a curated selection from a messier reality. For a virtual influencer, the highlight reel is the entire existence. There is no reality underneath it to select from.

At the extreme end: **De Coninck** (2026) documents AI-generated personas posing as military personnel, nurses, and police officers — using the uniform as a trust signal and the follower's automatic social responses as the entry point to disinformation, financial harm, and extremist content. The same article reports virtual influencers appearing alongside **President Trump** — the commercial trust mechanism borrowed for political signalling. Same mechanism. Different deployment.

**Why This Matters:** The virtual influencer makes visible what the [[Influencer]] form always contained: trust is the product. The virtual influencer makes it explicit — by engineering trust directly, without a person involved.
