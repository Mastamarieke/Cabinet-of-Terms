***This is not about one man. It is about what one man's accumulation of infrastructure reveals about the system that allowed it.***

> A governance model in which private infrastructure, public dependence, and personal authority combine — without the accountability structures that normally accompany power at this scale.

**Literal meaning:** **Muskism** is Quinn Slobodian and Ben Tarnoff's term, introduced in 2026, for the governance model represented by **Elon Musk**: the accumulation of critical public infrastructure under private control, the use of that control to extract political influence, and the deployment of that influence to further reduce the regulatory constraints on private power.

**Origin:** Slobodian and Tarnoff published "**Muskism**" as an analytical concept to name a governance logic that preceded Musk but found its clearest embodiment in him: private ownership of communications infrastructure (X/Twitter), space access infrastructure (SpaceX), payment infrastructure (X Money), cognitive interface infrastructure ([[Neuralink]]), and direct government advisory roles — a concentration of infrastructure dependency that has no democratic accountability.

**The Appeal:** **Muskism** offers an argument for its own legitimacy: the private sector builds better infrastructure faster, [[Objectivism (Rand)|exceptional individuals]] generate more value than bureaucratic institutions, and regulatory constraints on innovation damage public welfare. These arguments have empirical support in specific contexts. The question is whether they justify the governance outcome.

**The Friction:** **Muskism** begins with a dependency that no one voted on. In 2022 Ukraine asked SpaceX to switch on Starlink as far as Sevastopol for an attack on the Russian fleet; Musk refused, as he confirmed on X after Walter Isaacson's biography appeared in 2023 ([Lyngaas, 2023](https://www.cnn.com/2023/09/07/politics/elon-musk-biography-walter-isaacson-ukraine-starlink/index.html)). Two things make that power possible: [[Technofeudalism]], Yanis Varoufakis's term for platform owners who collect rent from users with no alternative, is the economic model of **Muskism**, and [[Broligarchy]], the network of tech owners bound by friendship and money, its social one. [[Flood the Zone]], filling the information space until accountability cannot keep up, is how it meets criticism. So far the strongest response has come from outside the US: in December 2025 the European Commission fined X €120 million under the Digital Services Act for its deceptive blue checkmark, its advertising archive and shutting out researchers ([Nicol-Schwarz & Kolodny, 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/05/elon-musks-x-fined-140-million-by-european-commission.html)), Europe's rules reaching a platform owned elsewhere, the [[Brussels Effect]]. Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor count Musk among the tech bosses of [[Endtime Fascism]]: in their reading he set up SpaceX partly to watch an AI apocalypse from space, and he funds parties such as the AfD ([Bahara, 2026](https://www.volkskrant.nl/buitenland/eindfascisten-als-musk-geloven-dat-het-paradijs-op-aarde-alleen-mogelijk-is-na-de-totale-destructie-ervan-ze-proberen-het-zelfs-te-bespoedigen~b098f075/)). [[Technofascism]] is the name for **Muskism** once it holds the state's means of coercion.

**Why This Matters:** **Muskism** names a governance logic, not a personality. Once you see it as a model rather than an individual, you can identify it wherever the same accumulation of infrastructure dependency, political influence, and accountability evasion appears.
